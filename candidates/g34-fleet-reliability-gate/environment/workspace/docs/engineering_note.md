# Engineering note — assembly life and the case for a longer overhaul interval

From: Reliability Engineering

We are assessing whether the 24-month overhaul interval can be extended. The interval was set in
2019 from bench data and has not been revisited against field experience.

The question we are asking of the fleet history is a property of the **assembly**, not of the
maintenance programme: how long does a critical assembly last in service before it fails? Our
overhaul practice removes assemblies from service at 24 months whether or not they were close to
failing, and units leave the fleet for commercial reasons, so the field record is incomplete for
this purpose in the ordinary way — we do not see the failure that would have happened later.

Provided the overhaul schedule and the retirement decision are driven by the SOP interval and by
site economics rather than by the condition of the individual assembly — which the SOP says they
are, and which we can check against pre-removal vibration history — the incomplete records can be
handled in the standard way and the underlying assembly life is recoverable.

Aftermarket are asking a different question for the FY27 build. Ours is not that question and our
number should not be used for theirs.
