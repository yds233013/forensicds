# Residential time-of-use tariff - programme note

**Owner:** Regulatory & Rates
**Status:** approved, mandatory from 1 May 2027

## What changes

Every residential customer moves from the flat energy rate to a time-of-use rate. The peak window
(17:00-21:00) is priced at roughly three times the off-peak rate. There is no opt-out.

## What does not change

- The physical distribution network, its capacity, and its losses.
- The relationship between outdoor temperature and cooling load. A tariff changes when customers
  choose to run equipment; it does not change how much cooling a building needs at a given
  temperature.
- Customer counts, premises, or the customer mix.

## What Load Research expects

Customers shift discretionary consumption - pool pumps, laundry, EV charging, pre-cooling - out of
the peak window. The extent varies a great deal by premises type and by what equipment can actually
be scheduled. A home with a controllable thermostat and a pool pump timer has far more it can move
than an apartment with a window unit.

Load Research also note that the scope for shifting is not constant through the season. See
`load_research_note.md`.
