### Analysis of the Yield Drop

The reported nonconforming rate rose from a baseline of **2.75%** (weeks 13–18) to **6.69%** (weeks 19–24), an increase of **3.94 percentage points**. However, the "step" in week 19 coincided exactly with a calibration event on **CMM-1** (`2026-05-04`), which introduced a significant measurement offset.

*   **Measurement System Effect:** Using the retained-artefact programme (QP-07 §5), I established a **+7.81 µm** offset for CMM-1. Because CMM-1 reads "large," it has been incorrectly dispositioning parts as `FAIL` when they are actually within the ±30 µm tolerance. This accounts for **2.88 percentage points** of the reported nonconforming rate.
*   **Material Effect:** After correcting for the instrument offset, the nonconforming rate for the post-window is **3.81%**. The increase attributable to the change in heat family (H2 to H3) is approximately **1.06 percentage points** (3.81% corrected vs. 2.75% baseline).

### Restatement (Schedule 3 §3.2)

Per the requirements of the supply quality agreement:

*   **Window:** Weeks 19–24 (inclusive).
*   **Population:** 12,000 parts machined and inspected during the window.
*   **Reference:** Conformance reference established per QP-07 §3 (corrected for machine offsets).
*   **Nonconforming Rate:** The rate measured against the conformance reference is **3.81%**.

### Recommendation

**Withdraw the escalation to change supplier.** Schedule 3 §3.2 stipulates that escalation may only be pursued if the material-attributable nonconforming rate, measured against the conformance reference, exceeds **5.5%**. The evidence shows a corrected rate of **3.81%**, which is well below the threshold. The matter should be redirected to the corrective-action process in Schedule 5, focusing on the material's interaction with the process and the immediate remediation of the CMM-1 instrument offset.
