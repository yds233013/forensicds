## 1. EXECUTIVE VERDICT

**The tasks are real; the story about them is partly wrong.**

Three findings, in order of importance.

1. **The dominant failure mode is not F9.** The report claims 10 of 13 failed final-suite trials are
   "right method family, wrong statistical object". Re-derived trial by trial, **4–5 trials meet the
   F9 evidentiary bar** (G24 ×3, G05 rsDKTXQ, plus G08 h7TpDUG outside the final five). The dominant
   labels are **F1** — committing to an identification assumption without ever testing it (G05 ×3,
   G10 ×2) — and **F4** — correct diagnosis, semantically wrong repair (Task02 ×3, Task04 ×2, G34 ×1,
   G41 ×2). The distinction matters: F9 says the agent aimed at the wrong target; F1/F4 say it aimed
   correctly and never checked whether it had hit.
2. **The no-falsification finding is stronger than claimed, and it is the real result.** Across **45
   valid trials on 15 tasks**: zero placebo tests, zero pre-trend or parallel-trends checks, zero
   negative controls, zero held-out or synthetic-censoring back-tests, zero simulations with a known
   answer. One genuine falsification-class check exists in the entire corpus (G10 `cLtM9yi` using the
   randomised holdout arms) and it was used to confirm a diagnosis, never to test the trial's own
   estimator. Several *falsification-shaped* checks were computed at the same wrong grain as the
   estimate and therefore certified the defect — the sharpest being G24 `wVmAykK` calling a comparison
   "astonishingly close… highly accurate" when both sides carried the identical unit error.
3. **The benchmark's difficulty is explained by one design property, not by the slice name.** Every
   task whose scientific object is **stated somewhere in the workspace** was solved (Task03, Task05,
   Task06, G35, G42 at 3/3; Task01 at 2/3). Every task whose object must be **derived from how the
   data came to exist** was unsolved (Task02, G05, G10, G24 at 0/3) — with G34 the single exception,
   and G34 was solved in ~20 steps for $0.04 by two trials that never queried the warehouse at all.

Consequences. The five-task suite is sound and its 1/5 pass@3 is genuine — all 13 failures reproduce
from the agents' own submitted code, with zero environment friction in any of them. But three of the
twelve pilot tasks are documentation-lookup exercises, the report's §5–§6 claims need correction, and
two items must be disclosed that currently are not: the Task02 explicit-invariant variant is
**contradicted by its own verifier** (not in the final five), and G36's "0/3" would be **2/3 under a
correct grader**.

**Recommendation: stop task generation, ship the five, and rewrite the failure analysis.** See §23.

| F2 insufficient reconciliation | 0 | 1 (g10 LhEU3ny secondary) | 3 |
| F8 task/verifier defect | 0 | **0** | 2 outside the five (G36 ×2 trials; Task02 variant ×3 trials) |

**Proposed revisions to the taxonomy.**

1. **Tighten F9.** Require documented evidence that the method family was substantially right *and*
   name the wrong object axis. Under that bar, half the current F9 population is F4 or F1.
2. **Restore F1 as a first-class label.** The G05 and G10 failures are not object errors at all: the
   population, grain, weighting and window were right and the *identifying assumption* (parallel trends
   across formats; that an average day stands in for this day) was adopted and never tested.
3. **Add F11 — coherence stopping.** The agent runs a check, obtains agreement, and treats agreement as
   confirmation when the check has no discriminating power. This is not F7 ("no validation"); it is
   validation with zero power, and it is what actually terminated most trials. Evidence: g24 wVmAykK
   ("astonishingly close… highly accurate" — both sides at the same wrong grain); g08 h7TpDUG (January
   pack matched at 1 dp with every actual drawn from the wrong run type); task02 nXXMdDm/pf9zaPc (AUC
   0.7697/0.7698 accepted against a correct 0.7806); task04 3SUW3gh (reproduced six published quarters
   exactly by adopting the rule that produced them); g10 all three (stopped when ice cream turned
   positive — the sign the brief had already supplied).
4. **Add a disclosure axis, not a label.** In three trials the agent stated a guarantee it had not
   implemented (g41 RZW9reM: "Implements a Min-Cost Max-Flow… ensures the minimum achievable

**Would a decision-only verifier have passed these?** Yes — **6 of 13 failed final-suite trials**, plus
both G41 failures. On G24 a decision-only grader would have scored the task 3/3 instead of 0/3.

**What it implies for methodology.** Decision robustness is a property of the *decision rule's margin*,
not evidence that the analysis is sound. Where the rule has a wide band (G10's ±5 pp, G41's 40-unit
threshold) moderate object error survives; where it is tight (G34's 0.28 trigger, G05's 2.5 % hurdle)
the same class of error flips it. A benchmark that grades only the recommendation measures the
generator's choice of threshold as much as the agent. **Grading the quantities beneath the decision is
the single most defensible methodological choice in this project**, and this audit strengthens the
evidence for it.

## 11. RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN THE FAILURE MODES

**They are not independent, and the ordering is visible in the trajectories.**

The evidence supports a **sequence with a specific mechanism**:

1. **A wrong commitment is made early** — either to an object (F9) or to an identifying assumption
   (F1) — typically within the first third of the trajectory, often in a single code-writing step
   (g05 step 34/40/36; g10 step 38/54/30; g24 step 24/28/36).
2. **The checks that follow cannot discriminate**, because they are computed *inside* the same
   commitment. This is the causal link, and it is mechanical rather than psychological: g24 wVmAykK's
   IPS estimate and its on-policy comparator were both at serve grain, so the comparison could not
   fail; g08 h7TpDUG's January pack cells matched at 1 dp with every actual drawn from the wrong run
   type; task04's added filter reproduced the published workbook *because the workbook was generated
   with that filter*. Coherence checks computed within a wrong frame are guaranteed to agree.
3. **Agreement is read as confirmation and the trial stops** (the proposed F11).
4. **The decision is then right or wrong depending on the decision rule's margin** (F10), which is
   independent of everything above.

Evidence for the ordering rather than mere co-occurrence: in **four trials the agent named the correct
consideration and then discarded it** — g05 rsDKTXQ named kit transport and chose the pooled figure
because the decision was "safe"; g10 LhEU3ny named the forecast circularity and then used forecasts as
its prior "to handle edge cases elegantly"; g10 eMXZbBi predicted the exact explosion its estimator
would suffer and shipped it uncapped; g41 G6PJWX4 worked out the greedy counterexample in a source
comment and chose greedy "for simplicity". Hypothesis *generation* is not the bottleneck; the bottleneck
is that nothing in the agent's loop forces a generated hypothesis to be tested.

**Uncertainty.** This is 45 trials of one model on 15 tasks built by one author. The sequence is
consistent with every trajectory read, but steps 1→2 could also be explained by a single underlying
trait (low propensity to seek disconfirming evidence) that produces both the early commitment and the
weak checks. Distinguishing those would need an intervention — e.g. a condition that supplies the
falsification route explicitly — which this project has not run.

## 12. DIFFICULTY COMPARISON

**What does not explain difficulty** (measured, §3 of `quantitative_evidence.md`):
- **Evidence volume.** 0/3 tasks span 15–34 workspace files; 3/3 tasks span 12–24.
- **Evidence heterogeneity.** 0/3 tasks have 4–7 evidence categories; 3/3 tasks have 4–6.
- **Warehouse complexity.** G10 (0/3) has 11 tables; G34 (2/3) and G24 (0/3) both have 4.
- **Investigation effort.** Every 0/3 task sits at 60–84 median steps — the model worked *harder* on the
  tasks it failed. Task04's pass ran the fewest steps of its three; G34's passes ran 18–20 steps.

**What does explain it** — one property, with one exception:

> **Is the scientific object stated anywhere in the workspace, or must it be derived from how the data
> came to exist?**

| | object stated | object must be derived |
|---|---|---|
| solved (≥1 of 3) | task03, task05, task06, g35, g42 (3/3); task01 (2/3); g44 (2/3, 3/3 on the object); g41 (1/3, object stated, **execution** unstated) | **g34 (2/3)** |
| unsolved (0/3) | — | task02, g05, g10, g24 |

**Why Task03/05/06/G35 were solved and Task02/G05/G10/G24 were not.** In the first group the decisive
sentence is in a document the agent reads anyway: Task03's monitoring doc states the selective-labels
rule and *the instruction names the exploration holdout*; Task05's pre-registration plan states all
seven required properties and the instruction names the file; Task06's contract and vendor doc state
the attribution, close and revision rules and the issued ledger contains worked examples of the target
output; G35's **output contract names all three estimands and defines the spillover arms verbatim**. In
the second group nothing states that identification is conditional on format (G05), that demand must be
a posterior given the day's observed sales (G10), that the propensity is over the eligible rather than
the retrieved pool and that a decision spans cached re-serves (G24), or that availability means load
time rather than commit time and that state is keyed per example (Task02).

**Was the difference "long-horizon scientific reasoning"? No — it is narrower.** All four unsolved
tasks were *fully reconstructed* at the operational-state layer: every G05 trial produced a byte-exact
analysis panel on all four extracts; every G10 trial passed the history-structure test including the
exact in-stock identity; every G24 trial rebuilt the exploration stream. The failure is localised to
**one step**: choosing the counterfactual (G05), choosing what the expectation is conditional on (G10),
choosing the action space and decision unit (G24), choosing the availability clock and the state key
(Task02). That is *estimand and identification selection*, not horizon length.

**The G34 exception matters.** It is the only unsolved-class object that was solved, and both passes
did it in ~20 steps with **zero queries against the warehouse**. Its object — Kaplan–Meier vs cumulative
incidence under competing risks — is a single textbook substitution that the workspace prose describes
in plain language. It is best read as evidence that "derived" is necessary but not sufficient: the
derivation must not be a one-step recall of a named estimator.

| **F. Decision-Grade Analytics Repair** (proposed) | all 15, with an admission test that would have rejected 4 of them | — | **recommended** |

**Recommended slice.**

> **Decision-grade analytics repair.** An organisation already computes a number; a named decision with
> a stated rule rides on it; the number is wrong for a scientific reason rather than a crash. The agent
> must repair the pipeline that produces it, produce the correct quantity, and defend it.

It describes **design**, not failures, and gives an author four checkable admission tests *before* any
model is run:

1. Is there an incumbent artefact producing a specific wrong number?
2. Does a named decision with a stated rule ride on it?
3. **Is the correct object underdetermined by the workspace documents** — must it be derived from how
   the data came to exist, or from an optimisation the documents do not solve?
4. Is the correct answer deterministic given the extract, and are the plausible wrong answers separated
   from it by more than the reporting precision?

Test 3 is the one this audit adds, and it is the test Task03, Task05, Task06, G35 and G42 fail. It also
preserves the second difficulty axis (G41: object stated, optimum unstated), which framing E would lose.

## 15. IN-SCOPE / OUT-OF-SCOPE for the recommended slice

**In scope.** Tabular enterprise systems of record (SQLite warehouses, event logs, ledgers, landing
zones); an incumbent pipeline in Python or SQL that the agent must repair; heterogeneous supporting
evidence (policy docs, SOPs, contracts, prior reports, notebooks, incident notes, configs, deployment
logs) including evidence that is *wrong but authoritative-sounding*; causal, statistical and
operational-research estimands; deterministic latent truth with hidden regimes; a decision rule with a
stated threshold; grading of the quantities beneath the decision as well as the decision.

**Out of scope.** Leaderboard model training; unstructured or multimodal inputs; visualisation;
open-ended "insight" questions needing an LLM judge; tasks whose correct object is stated in a document
the agent reads anyway (**new**); tasks where the decision rule's margin is so wide that any moderate
error preserves the decision (**new**, from the F10 audit); tasks whose accepted answer family is
narrower than the analysis it claims to test (**new**, from the G10 mixing-distribution caveat).

   scored those as successes.
7. The measured difficulty is concentrated at **estimand and identification selection**, not at
   evidence volume, heterogeneity or horizon length: every 0/3 task was reconstructed correctly at the
   operational-state layer and failed at one identifiable step.
8. **Task difficulty tracks one design property**: whether the correct object is stated in the workspace
   or must be derived from how the data came to exist (15/15 tasks consistent, one exception, G34).
9. Engineering cost per task fell about **2.5×** (≈3,500 → ≈1,000 Python lines) as the harness became

## 20. WHAT WE CANNOT CLAIM

1. **Not** that F9 is the dominant failure mode (4–6 trials under a strict bar, not 10/13).
2. **Not** that "G05: 2/3, stop was correct" — only one of three G05 trials is decision-correct on every
   graded extract.
3. **Not** that the suite measures "long-horizon" capability. Nothing here measures horizon: the failures
   occur early, the agents used a fraction of their budget, and the hardest tasks were not the longest.
4. **Not** that these tasks represent professional multi-hour work. The declared 90–300 minute estimates

9. **Not** that the 20 % pass@3 would hold for a stronger model, a different agent harness, or more trials.

## 21. MERCOR / APEX TRANSFERABILITY

**Transfers.** (a) The methodological finding that decision-only grading passes analyses whose every
quantity is wrong — 6 of 13 here — and the corresponding case for **scientific-chain scoring**. (b) The
falsification-absence result, which is model-behavioural rather than task-specific. (c) The design rule
that difficulty requires the object to be *underdetermined by the artefacts*, with the natural
experiment that supports it (five stated-object tasks solved, four derived-object tasks not). (d) The
pre-build gate discipline: wrong-object panels, separation ratios, counterexample search, Oracle/Nop,
mutation suites — roughly half of well-motivated designs fail these, and catching a defect after a
baseline costs a contaminated result (G36). (e) The F8-detectability rule: a verifier defect is
invisible in the reasoning trace and visible as a *constant ratio* residual concentrated in one graded
field.

**Does not transfer.** Absolute pass rates (one model, one harness, n = 3); Harbor-specific mechanics;
tolerance calibrations; anything about horizon length, which this project does not measure.

**Genuinely multi-hour-professional-like tasks, on this evidence:** G05, G08, G10, G41, Task02, Task06.
Everything else is under an hour of expert work as built.

**What would be needed for APEX-style professional realism.** Human baselines under measurement;
**shared enterprise worlds carrying several incidents** so that state built for one is reused by
another (currently each task is an isolated world, which is the single biggest gap versus real work);
scientific-chain scoring with partial credit at the object, estimator and decision layers separately;
and tasks where the analyst must *choose between two defensible estimators and defend the choice*,
which no task here requires.

**Expert roles needed to author and validate.** A causal-inference methodologist; a domain operator per
vertical (retail planning, settlement operations, field service, recsys serving); a data engineer for
the point-in-time and vintage machinery; and an independent adversarial reviewer whose only job is the
"is the object stated?" test.

**Claims currently too weak for a fellowship proposal:** anything about long-horizon capability; any
human-vs-model comparison; generalisation beyond one model; and "these tasks are multi-hour
professional work", which is an author estimate.

## 22. MISSING DIMENSIONS

Scientifically meaningful dimensions **absent from the final five**:

1. **Constrained decision optimisation / feasible-set reconstruction** — build and *solve* a feasible
   set (G41 exists and is 1/3, but is not in the five). The only demonstrated second difficulty axis.
2. **Economic-object non-linearity** — money that is not linear in quantity (allowances, tiers,
   penalties), so a correct quantity delta still gives the wrong amount. Task06 has it; the five do not.
3. **Estimator choice under two defensible options** — a task where the analyst must justify a choice
   rather than find the one right answer, graded on the justification's consequences.
4. **A required falsification artefact** — a contract that grades a diagnostic (a pre-trend table, a
   negative control, a back-test) alongside the estimate. Given finding 4 in §19, this is the single
   most informative addition available.
5. **Deployment-population transport where the target population is genuinely unobserved** — G44 came
   close but stated the target rate in the contract.
6. **Multi-incident shared world** — two or three incidents over one enterprise world, where state built
   for the first is reused by the second.
7. **Hierarchical aggregation with a real reconciliation constraint** — bottom-up vs top-down totals that
   must tie, rather than a filter-and-groupby (G42 attempted this and became a reading task).
8. **Forecasting/planning state** — G08 covers vintages and is not in the five; nothing in the five
   involves a forward-looking plan.

## 24. IF MORE TASKS ARE WARRANTED

Capabilities they should test — not designs:

1. **Reconstruct a feasible set and compute an optimum over it**, where no document solves the
   optimisation and a natural greedy looks correct. (Demonstrated to work: G41, 2/3 failures.)
2. **Produce a falsification artefact as a graded deliverable** — a pre-trend table, a negative control
   or a back-test that must be correct alongside the estimate.
3. **Choose between two defensible estimators and live with the consequences**, where both are
   accepted but they imply different decisions and the extract contains the evidence that resolves it.
4. **Carry a quantity through a non-linear economic mapping** (allowances, tiers, penalties) so a
   correct quantity still yields the wrong money.
5. **Transport an estimate to a population that is not observed** and whose composition must itself be
   reconstructed, not read from a contract field.
6. **Sustain state across two incidents in one enterprise world.**

**Admission test every candidate must pass before build** (from §14): incumbent artefact + named
decision rule + **object underdetermined by the documents** + deterministic truth with wrong answers
separated beyond reporting precision + a decision margin narrow enough that object error changes the
decision.
