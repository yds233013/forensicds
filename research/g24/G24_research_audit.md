# G24 research and statistical-validity audit (before any implementation)

Inputs re-read: `research/gen3_candidate_pool.md` §G24, `research/gen3_designs/G24_recommender_ope.md`,
`research/gen3_design_tournament.md` (§3 findings 8–9, §4 row G24, §5), `research/gen3_implementation_shortlist.md`
§S2, `research/gen3_tournament_sims/reviewer_g24.py`.

Nothing is built. No model has been run. The Phase-0 simulation gate (`G24_phase0_gate.md`) must pass before any task
file is written.

## 1. What real workflow is modelled?

Offline evaluation of a ranking policy before launch: a recommendation team wants to know what a candidate ranker
would have been worth had it served traffic, using logs generated by the production ranker plus a small randomized
exploration stream. This is the standard "offline gate before an A/B" step in recommender teams, and the standard
failure is that logged outcomes are policy-dependent.

## 2. Who does this work?

A recommender/personalisation data scientist or applied scientist, with an experimentation partner. The repair is a
production evaluation job that the team re-runs on future extracts, not a one-off notebook.

## 3. What decision depends on it?

Which ranker serves the home row next quarter: keep production (v6), ship v7 (already A/B tested, disputed), or ship
v7_pd (never tested online). The launch rule is written in the workspace: ship the candidate whose lift over production
has a 95% lower bound above zero; if both qualify, the larger estimate.

## 4. Estimand

For target policy π, the expected home-row clicks per **slate decision** over the decisions in the extract window:

> V(π) = E_x[ Σ_{k=1..5} θ_device(k) · r(u, π(x)_k) ]

- x is a decision context: user, device, surface, time, retrieved candidate pool, eligibility state.
- π(x)_k is the item that policy π would place in slot k **after the same post-ranking eligibility filter the serving
  stack applies**.
- The outcome is clicks attributable to the decision, counted as binary per (decision, slot), zeros included for slots
  never viewed.
- The population is every slate decision in the extract, all devices and all streams, not per served response, not per
  render, and not conditioned on views.

Reported quantities: V(v6), V(v7), V(v7_pd), lifts against v6, 95% intervals, and the launch decision.

## 5. Logging policy

Three streams, all sharing one retrieval stage:
- **prod_v6** (deterministic): serve the top 5 of the eligible pool by v6 score. Logged propensity 1.0.
- **explore_shuffle** (randomized, session-hashed): shuffle the retrieved pool uniformly, then the eligibility filter
  removes ineligible items from the ordered list, and the first five survivors are served.
- **ab_v7** (deterministic, a bounded window): top 5 eligible by v7 score. Logged propensity 1.0.

## 6. What was actually randomized?

The **ordering of the eligible pool for one slate decision**, once per (session, surface) cache group. Not the served
response: within the cache TTL, repeated requests re-serve the same stored slate, so a re-serve carries no new
randomization. Exploration assignment itself is by session hash, before A/B assignment.

## 7. At what grain is the propensity defined?

- **Logged field:** the ordered-slate probability under the shuffle over the **pre-filter** pool of size K:
  `1/(K(K−1)(K−2)(K−3)(K−4))`. It is computed in the ranker, before the business-rules layer runs.
- **What the data actually support:** because a uniform shuffle followed by deletion of ineligible items leaves a
  uniform permutation of the **eligible** items, the served slate is a uniform draw over orderings of the eligible pool
  of size m ≤ K. So:
  - item-in-slot marginal = 1/m for every eligible item and every slot;
  - item-in-slate marginal = 5/m;
  - ordered-slate probability = (m−5)!/m!.
- **Consequence:** the logged field is wrong in two ways at once (wrong stage, wrong grain), while still looking
  well-formed and reproducible from `candidate_count`. The correct quantity must be reconstructed from the serving
  code plus the per-candidate filter state in the logs.

This replaces the original design's propensity floor. One mechanism that makes the field unusable is enough, and a
field that looks valid is a better attractor than one that is obviously broken (a constant 0.01).

## 8. What outcomes are observed?

Client events: render, view (slot scrolled into view) and click, keyed by serve and page view, with slot and item.
The decision-level outcome is the binary indicator that the item in that slot was clicked in any render of the
decision.

## 9. Under what conditions are they observed?

- Only for served slates: counterfactual slots are never observed, which is what makes this an off-policy problem.
- Slots below the fold are viewed only when scrolled (device-dependent), but a never-viewed slot is a real zero for the
  decision value, not a missing value.
- Clicks are attached to the serve that rendered them, so a decision's clicks can be spread over several serves.

## 10. Identifiability assumptions

- **A1 (randomization).** Within the exploration stream, the served ordering is uniform over eligible-pool orderings,
  independent of potential outcomes given the context. Documented by the serving code; checkable in the logs (uniform
  slot distribution by pool rank).
- **A2 (no interference).** One decision's slate does not affect another decision's outcomes. Exploration assignment
  is session-level, so within-session carryover is inside the decision unit.
- **A3 (consistency).** The click that would occur under a counterfactual slate at slot k equals the observed click for
  that item at that slot: the position-based click model (examination by slot × device, independent slots). Slot-exact
  IPS does not need A3 beyond consistency of the whole slate; position-transfer estimators do.
- **A4 (same downstream stack).** A counterfactual policy's slate is subject to the same eligibility filter, so the
  target slate is the first five eligible items in its ranking.
- **A5 (decision unit).** Re-serves within the TTL carry the stored slate; the decision's value is the expected clicks
  over the decision, not per serve. Serve counts are post-treatment (worse slates cause more reloads) and must not
  enter the weighting.

## 11. Overlap / positivity

- Retrieval is shared by all policies, and every candidate policy re-ranks the same pool, so every target slate is a
  permutation of the eligible pool: every target item-slot pair has logging probability 1/m > 0 under exploration.
  **Positivity holds by construction**, which keeps the task about semantics rather than about unbounded weights.
- Production and A/B streams have propensity 1 for their own slate and 0 for any other: they identify only their own
  policy's value (useful as on-policy validation, never as off-policy support).
- Weight range: m ∈ [6, 16] in the visible regime, so weights are bounded and clipping is never necessary. An agent that
  clips anyway introduces bias that the graded values detect.
- Exploration is a random session sample, so exploration decisions are representative of the population; no device
  reweighting is needed (reweighting by device share is also valid).

## 12. Real operational behaviour that complicates the logs

| Behaviour | Real? | Statistical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Post-ranking eligibility filter (already-watched suppression, licence windows) runs after the ranker logs its propensity | yes, ubiquitous | logged propensity refers to the wrong universe; target slates must be filtered too |
| Server-side slate cache with a TTL; reloads re-serve the same slate | yes | serve ≠ randomization; serve counts are post-treatment and correlate with slate quality |
| Exploration on a session-hashed traffic slice | yes | defines the estimable population; random subsample |
| Position-dependent examination, device-specific | yes | item-anywhere credit is biased in a policy-dependent direction |
| Below-the-fold slots viewed only on scroll | yes | invites view-conditioned CTR, which is not the estimand |
| A/B readout unit = serves, giving an SRM warning | yes | the institutional attractor: "the experiment is broken" |
| Candidate logging sampled for production traffic (cost) | yes | limits direct-method fits on production; not needed for the estimand |

## 13. Complications kept, and why each changes the statistics

1. **Post-ranking filter after propensity logging** → the central question "what does the logged propensity refer to".
2. **Cache TTL and reloads** → the randomization unit, and a post-treatment weighting trap.
3. **Position-based clicks with device-specific curves** → estimator must handle slot credit; pooled curves fail.
4. **Session-hashed exploration slice** → defines the evaluation population and gives the support story.
5. **Deterministic production and A/B streams** → on-policy validation targets (v6 and v7), which make the wrong
   estimators falsifiable inside the workspace.

## 14. Complications removed as artificial

| Removed | Reason |
|---|---|
| Propensity floor (SDK 4.2.0 / DSP-311) | redundant with the stage mismatch and turns a subtle attractor into an obvious broken field |
| `cache_hit` field appearing mid-window | version-dependent field presence is archaeology, not statistics; also a near-recipe for the dedupe rule |
| Client retry duplicating click events | with a binary per (decision, slot) outcome it changes nothing |
| Separate `view` semantics used as the impression denominator | kept only as the view-conditioned-CTR attractor; no extra grading |
| Three-way model-card claims (NDCG, etc.) as evidence | narrative noise; model cards state training data and objective only |
| 170 MB extract with 5.4M candidate rows | build and verifier cost; the sampled-candidate-logging design is realistic and ≤ 40 MB |
| "Novelty effect" hypothesis with a drifting daily chart | a second institutional attractor is not needed once the SRM one exists; drops generator complexity |

## 15. Design changes forced by the tournament findings

| Finding | Change |
|---|---|
| Uniform shuffle makes the marginal 1/M, textbook (finding 9) | the marginal is 1/m over the **post-filter eligible** pool, and m must be reconstructed; the logged field encodes the pre-filter slate probability |
| `unpaired_ci` would pass (finding 8, unpaired SE ≈ 1.08× paired) | dropped as a mutation; intervals are graded only by a width band and a widened-coverage check |
| `slate_cache.yaml` is close to a recipe | the cache config states TTL and key only; no statement about decisions, clicks or evaluation; the glossary defines the KPI unit without saying how to reconstruct it |
| Extract too large | ≤ 40 MB SQLite: all exploration decisions, a 5% session sample of production, candidates logged for exploration plus 10% of production |
| Margins never simulated | Phase-0 gate before any build, with pre-registered tolerances, wrong-method separation and hidden-regime checks |
| Cheapest path 30–40 actions | the cheap path now yields the **wrong** universe (1/K), the wrong unit (serves) and position-unaware credit; each is separately detectable and separately graded |

## 16. Coherence check of the estimand and identifiability story

- The estimand is a policy value per decision, defined without reference to any estimator.
- It is identified from exploration decisions alone under A1–A5, with bounded weights and full positivity.
- It is estimable by at least four structurally different methods (slot-exact IPS, position-transfer IPS with
  estimated examination curves, doubly robust, and on-policy means where a stream exists), which the Phase-0 gate must
  confirm agree within tolerance.
- The verifier can grade below the decision: the reconstructed decision table, the counterfactual target slates, the
  values and intervals, and the launch decision.

**Conclusion: coherent. Proceed to Phase 0 (simulation gate), not to implementation.**
