# G36 simulation results

`simulation.py` (F1 design, truth, 15-method panel), `gates.py` (same-history, coherent-wrong,
cheap solves, recognition gate, leakage), `f2_repricing.py` (runner-up).
No model call, no container, no network. Runtime ~25 s total.

## 1. Truths and decisions

Gate: procure peaking capacity iff forecast mean peak load >= **2.900 kW**.

| regime | what differs | TRUTH | decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| `visible` | - | 2.9977 | **procure** |
| `hidden_a_strong_selection` | opt-in is far more skewed toward responsive homes | 2.9746 | **procure** |
| `hidden_b_flat_in_heat` | response barely fades with heat | 2.8205 | **defer** |
| `hidden_c_fades_in_heat` | response collapses in heat | 2.9429 | **procure** |
| `hidden_d_high_response` | broadly responsive population | 2.7075 | **defer** |

**3 procure / 2 defer.** Constant "procure" scores 3/5, constant "defer" 2/5.

perturbation.

## 3. Recognition-vs-execution gate — **PASS**

See `recognition_execution_gate.md`. Summary: H1 alone leaves 11.6 sd and 1/5 wrong decisions;
H2 alone leaves 10.6 sd and 2/5; both together reach 0.9 sd and 0/5.

## 4. Same-history / different-future — **PASS**

The historical period is generated by identical parameters in every regime. Only the tariff-response
mechanism differs, and the tariff did not exist historically.

| regime | holdout RMSE | holdout R2 | mean historical load | TRUTH | decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| visible | 0.7263 | 0.7470 | 2.9952 | 2.9977 | procure |
| hidden_a | 0.7300 | 0.7502 | 3.0250 | 2.9746 | procure |
| **hidden_b** | **0.7298** | 0.7568 | 3.0135 | **2.8205** | **defer** |
| **hidden_c** | **0.7486** | 0.7496 | 3.0633 | **2.9429** | **procure** |
| hidden_d | 0.7274 | 0.7500 | 3.0304 | 2.7075 | defer |

**The discriminating pair is `hidden_b` and `hidden_c`:** identical response magnitude, identical
history generation, differing only in how the response fades with heat. Holdout RMSE differs by
0.019 (2.6%, within seed noise); the correct forecasts differ by 0.12 kW and the decisions are
opposite. **No backtest can separate them. Only the pilot can.**

## 5. Coherent-but-wrong — **PASS**

`W1_historical_only` is the incumbent production model. It is not a strawman: it reconciles, it
calibrates, and it holds out at R2 0.747-0.757 on a genuine out-of-sample summer.

| regime | holdout R2 | forecast | truth | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| visible | 0.7470 | 3.3289 | 2.9976 | +0.3313 |
| hidden_a | 0.7502 | 3.3077 | 2.9748 | +0.3329 |
| hidden_b | 0.7568 | 3.2863 | 2.8202 | **+0.4661** |
| hidden_c | 0.7496 | 3.2640 | 2.9429 | +0.3211 |
| hidden_d | 0.7500 | 3.3454 | 2.7075 | **+0.6379** |

It is wrong about the future because the mechanism it omits **did not exist in the past**. No
historical diagnostic can detect that. This is the G34-baseline lesson in forecasting form: internal
coherence and out-of-sample historical performance are not validity for a changed regime.

## 6. Finalist comparison — why F2 lost

| | F1 TOU tariff | F2 repricing |
|---|---|---|
| accepted-estimator sd (relative to the quantity) | **0.5 %** | 8 % |
| worst decision margin | **3.5 sd** | 0.2 sd |
| H1 alone -> wrong decisions | 1 / 5 | **0 / 4** |
| H1 + H2 -> wrong decisions | **0 / 5** | **1 / 4** |

F2 fails on two counts: one insight alone already fixes every decision, and the accepted estimator
itself gets a decision wrong because its sampling sd is large relative to the gate margin. F1 wins
decisively on the criterion the tournament was built to test.

**A defect in my own F2 code, disclosed:** the first F2 run showed the "valid" estimator erring by
-1.46 while doing nothing erred by -0.64 - the accepted route was worse than the null. Cause was a
sign slip: `truth` computed `1 + (-e)*disc` while the estimators computed `1 + e*disc`. Fixed by
routing every estimator through one `index(e, disc)` helper. The comparison above is post-fix. Had I
not sanity-checked that the valid route should beat the null, F2 would have been rejected for the
wrong reason.
