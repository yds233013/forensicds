## 1. Research question

Can an agent recover a valid off-policy estimate of a ranking policy's online value when the logs are generated by
a deterministic production ranker plus a small randomized exploration stream, clicks depend on slot position,
the logged `propensity` field has slate-level semantics and was floored mid-window, and client reload behaviour
duplicates renders and clicks — and then make the correct launch decision among three policies, one of which was
never tested online?

The capability isolated is **estimand-preserving off-policy evaluation**: choosing the data that identifies a
counterfactual policy value (support), the propensity that matches the estimator's unit (item-in-slot vs slate), the
outcome model assumption that the click mechanism licenses (position), and the decision unit (slate decision, not
served response or client render), and validating the estimator against the on-policy ground truth that the logs
partially contain.

## 2. Enterprise setting

A video-streaming service's home page shows a five-slot "Top Picks" row on web, mobile and TV. The rec service
retrieves an eligible candidate list per request (12–30 titles; availability filtering by licence region), ranks
it with the production model v6, and caches the response per (session, surface) for 10 minutes. A 5% session-hashed
exploration stream serves a uniformly shuffled slate. Recs DS runs an offline gate (`recs_eval`) that replays
candidate models on sampled production logs; Experimentation runs online A/Bs. Two candidates exist: v7 (trained on
production click logs with exposure-count features) and v7_pd (trained with position debiasing on exploration logs).
Only v7 has been A/B tested.

## 4. Source distribution inspiration

- Offline replay of recommenders on logged data and its bias when the logging policy is not uniformly random is a
  well-known practitioner problem (replay method: Li et al., WSDM 2011 — to verify).
- Position bias and the examination (position-based) click model; intervention harvesting / randomization for
  estimating position propensities (Joachims et al., WSDM 2017; Agarwal et al., WSDM 2019 — to verify).
- Slate off-policy evaluation: item-position / pseudo-inverse estimators (Swaminathan et al., NeurIPS 2017; Li et
  al., "Offline evaluation of ranking policies with click models", KDD 2018 — to verify).
- Propensity clipping and self-normalised IPS as variance controls (Bottou et al., JMLR 2013; Swaminathan &
  Joachims 2015 — to verify).
- SRM caused by an analysis unit that is itself affected by treatment (page loads/serves rather than users) is a
  common experimentation pitfall (Kohavi, Tang & Xu, 2020 — to verify).

No claim is made about any real company.

## 5. Causal graph / ground truth

```
context x = (user u, device d, session, time) ──► candidate list C(x), |C| = M(x) ∈ [12, 30]
          │
policy π ─┴─► ordered slate (a_1..a_5) ──► examination θ_d(k) × relevance r(u, a_k) ──► click_k ∈ {0,1}
                                     │
slate value (clicks) ─► back-navigation / reload propensity ─► cache re-serves (same slate, new serve_id)
                                                         └─► client re-renders, view events, click-queue flush duplicates
```

**Contexts.** 2.6M slate decisions (cache misses) in the full window; the extract holds all 130k exploration
decisions and 5% session samples of production (~113k) and the v7 A/B arm (~14k). Device mix web 45% / mobile 30% /
TV 25%; TV share rises from 22% (early June) to 31% (late August).

**Click model (pure position-based model, independent across slots).**
P(click at slot k) = θ_d(k) · r(u, i), with
θ_web = [1.00, .70, .54, .43, .35], θ_mobile = [1.00, .62, .45, .33, .26], θ_tv = [1.00, .52, .33, .22, .15].
r(u,i) = 0.45 · σ(⟨p_u, q_i⟩ + b_i − 2.2), dimension 8, so r ∈ (0, 0.45). No cascade, no within-slate interaction —
a deliberate modelling choice (§27).

**Policies (target scores logged for every candidate).**
- v6: score = relevance-correlated signal + popularity term (Spearman with r ≈ 0.55 within candidate list).
- v7: trained on v6 logs with a position-confounded `item_ctr_28d` feature and a novelty boost; its top-5 **set** has
  higher summed relevance than v6 but its **ordering** within the slate is weakly anti-correlated with relevance
  (it promotes novel, moderately relevant titles to slots 1–2).
- v7_pd: well ordered, set relevance between v6 and v7.

**True values** V(π) = mean over all 2.6M decision contexts of Σ_k θ_d(k) · r(u, π(x)_k), computed exactly
(expectations, no click noise) by the generator:

| Policy | True value | vs v6 | Position-unaware value θ̄_d·Σr | Replay CTR@5 (gate) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v6 | 0.412 | — | 0.383 | 0.0824 |
| v7 | 0.398 | −3.4% | 0.421 | 0.0916 (+11.2%, coverage 31%) |
| v7_pd | 0.453 | +10.0% | 0.405 | 0.0871 (+5.7%, coverage 44%) |

**Logging.**
- `prod_v6` and `ab_v7` serves: deterministic top-5, `propensity = 1.0`.
- `explore_uniform` serves: `propensity` = 1/(M(M−1)(M−2)(M−3)(M−4)) (e.g. 5.4e-7 at M = 20) until logging SDK 4.2.0
  (2026-07-08, DSP-311), after which `propensity = max(p, 0.01)` = 0.01 for every exploration row.
- `candidate_count` = M(x) on every serve; `rec_candidates` lists the full candidate set with three model scores.

**Cache and client behaviour.**
- A home request within 600 s of the session's cache-miss serve (TTL from first serve, not refreshed on hit) gets the
  same slate under a new `serve_id`. Expected re-serves per decision: web 0.10, mobile 0.18, TV 0.40, each multiplied
  by (1 + 1.2·(0.45 − value_of_slate)/0.45) — worse slates cause more back-navigation.
- `cache_hit` column exists only from SDK 4.3.0 (2026-07-20); NULL before.
- A production session can legitimately receive an identical v6 slate as a **new decision** after the TTL expires
  (deterministic ranker, unchanged candidates): ~9% of production decisions have an identical-slate predecessor in
  the same session more than 600 s earlier.
- Each click is generated once per (decision, slot) and attached to one of the decision's renders (uniform over
  renders that viewed the slot). On a re-render the client's offline queue re-sends the last click event with the
  **new** `serve_id` (TV 50%, others 5%); it keeps its `client_event_id`.
- View events exist only for slots scrolled into view (TV slots 4–5 need a scroll, ~55% of TV renders).

**A/B (AB-1182, full data, report only).** 2026-08-04 → 08-24, user-hashed 50/50 among non-exploration sessions.
Readout unit = serves: v7 arm has 3.1% more serves (SRM p = 3e-11) because of reloads; clicks/serve −2.0%. User
counts balanced (p = 0.61); clicks per decision −3.2% (truth −3.4%). Daily effect drifts from −3.8% to −2.1% as TV
share rises (v7's deficit is smallest on TV).

## 6. Latent statistical/business invariant

1. **Unit.** The value unit is the slate decision = a cache-miss serve. Re-serves of the same slate within the cache
   TTL belong to the originating decision; the slot outcome is 1 if the user clicked that item in that slot in any
   render of the decision. Decision identity is (session, surface, first serve time, TTL), not slate content.
2. **Support.** Only the exploration stream has positive probability for arbitrary target slates. Production and A/B
   streams identify only their own policy's value (on-policy), never v7_pd's.
3. **Propensity semantics.** The logged `propensity` is the ordered-slate probability and is unusable after the floor.
   An unbiased estimator under the position-based click model needs the item-in-slot marginal, which for uniform
   exploration is 1/M(x) — reconstructed from the candidate count, not read from the field — or estimated position
   examination ratios plus item-in-slate marginals 5/M(x).
4. **Position.** Crediting a logged click to a target slot other than the logged slot requires dividing out θ_d(k′)
   and multiplying by θ_d(k) (device-specific), with θ identified from the randomized stream. Position-unaware
   credit is biased in a policy-dependent direction.
5. **Estimand.** Expected clicks per decision (zeros for unviewed slots), averaged over decisions of all devices — not
   view-conditioned CTR and not per-served-response CTR.
6. **Decision.** Launch the candidate whose lift over v6 has a 95% lower bound above 0; if both qualify, the higher
   estimate. Truth: v7_pd.

## 7. Grains and state variables

| Grain | Key | Where | Mistake that matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Served response | `serve_id` | `rec_serves` | treated as decision (TV overweight, duplicated clicks, SRM) |
| Slate decision | first `serve_id` of a cache group | reconstructed | merged across TTL expiry (identical v6 slates) |
| Render | `page_view_id` + `serve_id` | `client_events` | clicks conditioned on a specific render lost |
| Slot outcome | (decision, position) | reconstructed | view-conditioned CTR; duplicate flushed clicks |
| Candidate set | (serve_id, item_id) | `rec_candidates` | M assumed constant (20) |
| Logging era | SDK version | `logging_version`, CHANGELOG | floored propensity mixed with raw |
| Device position curve | device × position | estimated from exploration | pooled θ across devices |
| Policy stream | `policy_id` | `rec_serves` | production rows used as off-policy support |

## 11. Why each wrong hypothesis is plausible

- **H1** is the organisational default: an SRM warning is a textbook reason to distrust an experiment, and the gate
  is the team's own tooling. Real statistical evidence (a tiny p-value) supports it; refuting it requires recognising
  that the serve count is post-treatment.
- **H2** has a real-looking trend chart; novelty effects are common in recommenders. Refuting it requires device
  stratification — a composition explanation.
- **H4** is what an agent reaches after correctly discarding replay but using the easiest-looking fix: the
  notebook's clipped SNIPS gives values 0.383 / 0.421 / 0.405, internally consistent with the replay ordering, and
  *also* says v7 beats v6 — so an agent then sides with H1. This is the central attractor: two biased estimators agree.
- **H5** is plausible because the floor destroys the field; the agent must realise the field is not needed.

## 13. Natural wrong implementation

**NW1 — "use exploration traffic, fix the propensity, self-normalise"** (the most likely final state):

```python
exp = serves[serves.policy_id == "explore_uniform"]
imp = load_impressions(exp)                       # existing load.py: view events ⋈ clicks on (serve_id, item_id)
w = 1.0 / imp.propensity.clip(lower=0.01)         # the notebook's clipping
for model in ["v6", "v7", "v7_pd"]:
    top5 = candidates.top_k(model, 5)             # target slate per serve_id
    m = imp.merge(top5, on=["serve_id", "item_id"])   # item anywhere in slate
    value[model] = 5 * (m.clicked * w).sum() / (w * imp_per_serve).sum()   # SNIPS-style
```

What it gets wrong numerically:
- Item match ignores position → value ∝ θ̄·Σr: v6 0.383, v7 0.421, v7_pd 0.405 → launches **v7**, and "confirms" the
  replay, so the agent concludes the A/B is broken.
- Pre-floor rows have weights ~1e6 × M-dependent → SNIPS is dominated by pre-2026-07-08 decisions with large M;
  post-floor rows weigh 100. Believable but era-weighted.
- Serves instead of decisions: TV decisions counted 1.40× (with flushed duplicate clicks), shifting all values by
  about −0.006 to +0.004 depending on policy.
- View-joined impressions drop unviewed slots from the denominator in some variants (CTR among viewed): +0.03–0.06.

**NW2 — "fix replay"**: keep production-stream replay, require exact slot match. On-policy for v6 (0.412), but v7
and v7_pd are evaluated only where they agree with v6 → v7 0.47, v7_pd 0.44 (selection on agreement). Wrong launch.

## 14. Second-order failure modes

1. **Slot-exact IPS with a hard-coded 1/20** (M assumed constant from the docstring example): biased by E[M·…]
   covariance with relevance (larger candidate lists in big-catalogue regions): ≈ +0.012 on v7_pd in visible, larger
   in hidden_b (M 8–40). Fails hidden_b value tolerance.
2. **Correct item-in-slot IPS but on serves** (no decision reconstruction): values within tolerance on visible, but
   `slate_decisions.csv` wrong (≈ 31k extra decisions) and flushed clicks duplicated.
3. **Dedupe "keep first serve, drop the rest"**: loses clicks made after a reload: TV clicks −18%, values −0.02 to −0.03;
   table wrong.
4. **Dedupe by identical slate within session** (ignoring TTL): merges ~10k legitimate v6 decisions; on-policy v6
   cross-check shifts to 0.426; table wrong.
5. **PBM with θ pooled across devices**: θ̄ curve applied to TV decisions overcredits deep slots: v7_pd +0.02, v7 −0.01;
   borderline tolerance on visible, fails hidden_a (TV-heavy).
6. **Using on-policy A/B for v7, "no estimate possible" for v7_pd → keep v6**: wrong launch.
7. **Direct method only** (fit r per item on exploration, plug into target slates): user-level heterogeneity is not
   captured by item/device effects; bias ≈ −0.02 to −0.04 on v7_pd (under-rewards personalised ordering). Fails.
8. **Two-sample CI for lift** ignoring pairing: CI too wide (lower bound for v7_pd ≈ +0.004 — still qualifies on
   visible, fails hidden_a where the gap is smaller). The verifier's CI-width check rejects widths > 3× reference.

## 15. Correct repair properties

- Decisions reconstructed from serves by (session, surface, TTL from first serve), independent of `cache_hit`
  availability; clicks merged across renders per (decision, position), binary.
- Off-policy estimates for any target use only exploration decisions; production/A/B streams used only on-policy
  (optional) and for validation.
- Item-in-slot probability derived from the exploration mechanism and the decision's candidate count (or θ-ratio
  estimator with device-specific θ estimated from exploration), not from the logged field.
- Outcome = clicks per decision including zeros; population = all decisions (all devices) — exploration is a random
  session sample, so no reweighting by device is needed, but reweighting by device-share is also valid.
- Paired uncertainty for lifts; seeded; launch rule applied as written.

## 17. Validation requirements

Checks an agent needs (and which aggregates cannot replace):
- **Decision reconstruction at row level:** sample sessions with multiple serves; confirm re-serves share slots and fall
  within 600 s of the first; confirm identical v6 slates after TTL are separate; confirm post-4.3.0 `cache_hit` agrees
  with the reconstruction (it should, 100%). An aggregate (decision count) cannot show which serves were merged.
- **Propensity semantics:** on pre-floor exploration rows, logged propensity equals ∏ 1/(M−k+1) at logged M.

## 20. Alternative valid implementations

Any estimator that is unbiased or low-bias for the per-decision value under the documented mechanisms passes:
slot-exact item-in-slot IPS (with or without self-normalisation within exploration — SNIPS on correct weights is
consistent), PBM-based IPS with device-specific θ estimated from exploration, pseudo-inverse slate estimators, doubly
robust with any reward model, on-policy means where available. Decision reconstruction may use `cache_hit` where
present if it falls back to the TTL rule where absent. CIs may be bootstrap (seeded), delta-method, or
Wald on per-decision contributions. The verifier grades values against generator truth with tolerance, CIs by
coverage-in-widened-band and width band, the table exactly, and the decision exactly — never the method.

## 21. Verifier design

Pipeline runs as uid 65534 without `/tests` access; extract digests checked first.

**A. Integrity.** Extract, serving, models unchanged; command exits 0; outputs present with schema.

**B. `slate_decisions.csv` exact.** Row set on (decision_id, position) vs generator; `policy_id`, `device`, `item_id`,
`clicked` exact. `decision_id` = serve_id of the cache-miss serve; the verifier also accepts any decision_id if a
one-to-one mapping to generator decisions exists with identical slot content and outcomes (so agents may use their
own ids). `decided_at` within 1 s.

**C. Values.** For each policy π: |V̂(π) − V(π)| ≤ τ_V(π); |L̂(π) − L(π)| ≤ τ_L(π) for π ∈ {v7, v7_pd}.

**D. Intervals.** `ci_low ≤ value ≤ ci_high`; width w satisfies 0.25·w_ref ≤ w ≤ 3·w_ref, where w_ref =
2·1.96·SE_ref; truth inside [value − 2·(w/2), value + 2·(w/2)]. Same for lift intervals. This rejects degenerate and
unpaired intervals without requiring nominal 95% coverage on a single draw.

**E. Decision exact.** **F. Determinism** (byte-identical rerun). **G.** B–E on three hidden extracts.

### Variance analysis and tolerances

Let n_e = exploration decisions, M_i the candidate count, V_k(x) = θ_d(k)·r(u, π(x)_k).

*Slot-exact item-in-slot IPS* (least efficient accepted estimator): Y_i = Σ_k 1[a_ik = π(x_i)_k]·c_ik·M_i.
E[Y_i | x] = Σ_k V_k(x) (unbiased). E[Y_i² | x] = Σ_k M_i V_k(x) + Σ_{j≠k} M_i²/(M_i(M_i−1))·V_jV_k·… ≈ M_i·V(x)
+ V(x)². So Var(Y) ≈ E[M·V] − V² + E[V²] − V² ≈ 19 × 0.43 ≈ 8.2 for visible (sd ≈ 2.86).
With n_e = 130,000: **SE(V̂) ≈ 0.0079**.

*Lift* L = V(π) − V(v6) on the same decisions: Cov(Y_π, Y_v6) = E[Σ_k M·1[π_k = v6_k]·V_k]; slot-item agreement is
≈ 35% for v7_pd and ≈ 28% for v7, so Var(Y_π − Y_v6) ≈ 2 × 8.2 − 2 × 0.35 × 8.2 ≈ 10.7 → **SE(L̂) ≈ 0.0091**.

*PBM-IPS* (item anywhere, θ ratios, marginal 5/M): variance ≈ 0.3× slot-exact → SE ≈ 0.0045, SE(L̂) ≈ 0.005.

Generator computes SE_ref analytically for the slot-exact estimator per fixture and policy from the known θ, r and M
distribution (no simulation noise in the tolerance). Tolerances:
- τ_V = 3.0 · SE_ref(V) (visible ≈ 0.024), τ_L = 3.0 · SE_ref(L) (visible ≈ 0.027).
- **Seed screening (deterministic):** at build time the generator runs the reference panel (slot-exact IPS, PBM-IPS
  with estimated θ, DR, on-policy mix) on each fixture; a seed is accepted only if every panel estimate lies within
  2.5·SE_ref of truth and every panel launch decision equals the true decision. Under approximate normality each
  panel member violates 2.5σ with probability ≈ 1.2%; with correlated members the rejection rate is ≈ 3–5%. Seeds and
  panel results are recorded in `tests/fixture_audit.json`. The 0.5σ gap between screening (2.5σ) and grading (3σ)
  absorbs valid estimator variants outside the panel (different CI methods, SNIPS on correct weights).
- **Decision robustness:** with SE_ref(L) = 0.0091 and true lift 0.041 for v7_pd, the slot-exact lower bound falls
  below 0 only if L̂ < 0.0178, i.e. 2.5σ below truth; v7's true lift −0.014 qualifies only at +3.5σ. Fixture targets
  are set so every true decision has a ≥ 2.5σ margin for the least efficient estimator (hidden_a: lift 0.030 with
  n_e = 96k, SE ≈ 0.0106 → margin 2.8σ; screened).
- **Wrong estimators separate:** position-unaware credit misses v6 by −0.029 (3.7σ) and v7_pd by −0.048 (6σ) and
  flips the decision; replay ×5 misses v7 by +0.059. Where a wrong method lands inside τ_V (e.g. serves-as-decisions),
  the exact table or a hidden fixture catches it (§19).

Truth itself is exact (finite population of 2.6M generated contexts, expectations not draws), so there is no
reference-estimator error in the comparison.

## 27. Benchmark risks

- **Implementation cost: high.** Generator must produce consistent cache/client behaviour, three policies with
  controlled set-vs-order relevance, analytic SE, and a seed-screening panel (four estimators × four fixtures). Extract
  size (~170 MB) needs parquet and gz; image size and verifier runtime (panel is precomputed at build, not run in the
  verifier) must be watched. Verifier runtime: four pipeline runs on ≤ 400k decisions, ~2–4 min each.
- **Realism.** A pure PBM with independent slots is cleaner than real behaviour; a cascade component would make
  PBM-IPS biased and reintroduce estimator arbitration. Accepted: realism traded for gradability, stated in docs as
  nothing (the agent is not told the model), and slot-exact IPS remains valid regardless.
- **Gradability.** Tolerances are principled (3σ of the least efficient accepted estimator, analytic), but an unusual
  valid estimator with larger variance (e.g. pseudo-inverse with heavy slate-level variance) could fall outside on
  one of four extracts. Mitigation: include pseudo-inverse in the screening panel if its SE ≤ 1.3× slot-exact.
- **Overlap.** Task 03 (selective labels, exploration holdout) and Task 05 (SRM, analysis unit) are conceptually
  adjacent. Distinct here: estimator construction, propensity semantics, position, and grading against truth.
- **Headroom risk.** A strong agent that knows the OPE literature may go straight to position-aware IPS on
  exploration data. Remaining difficulty then sits in decision reconstruction (exact table) and hidden M/θ variation.
  If trials show fast passes, strengthen by making exploration a top-K-restricted shuffle (support deficiency for
  deep candidates, requiring a stated support check) — not done now to avoid underspecification.
- **Leakage via the A/B.** The per-decision A/B value for v7 is an exact validation target; that is intended.
- **Wrong-cause narratives** (e.g. "novelty") are not graded; the decision and values carry the grade.
