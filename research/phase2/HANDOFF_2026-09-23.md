## 2. RESEARCH QUESTION

> **Can a frontier data-science agent distinguish "my analysis is internally coherent" from "my scientific
> interpretation is actually valid"?**

Operationally: in a production incident where several interpretations are initially defensible, where the
workspace contains evidence whose outcome differs across those interpretations, and where a named decision
with a stated threshold rides on the answer — does the agent obtain the evidence that could refute the
interpretation it has already adopted?

The question is deliberately *not* "can agents do statistics". Phase 1 established that recognition and
execution are largely competent. The question is about the epistemic loop: **selection of discriminating
evidence**, and the stopping rule that ends an investigation.

## 3. DISCOVERY-SET FINDING

The 15 measured tasks and 45 valid target-model trials are the **discovery set**. The audit of 2026-09-23
corrected the project's own earlier interpretation. What survives:

**Established.**
1. **Recognition is not the bottleneck.** In all 13 failed final-suite trials the agent named the broad
   problem class correctly.
2. **Failures localise to a commitment step, not an execution step.** All four 0/3 tasks were reconstructed
   correctly at the operational-state layer — G05's analysis panel was byte-exact on all four extracts in all
   four trials; every G10 trial passed the in-stock identity; every G24 trial rebuilt the exploration stream.
   The failure is at choosing the counterfactual, the conditioning set, the action space and decision unit,
   or the availability clock.
3. **Near-absence of discriminating tests.** Across 45 valid trials: **0 placebo tests, 0 pre-trend or
   parallel-trends checks, 0 negative controls, 0 held-out/back-test validations, 0 known-answer
   simulations.** One trial (G10 `cLtM9yi`) ran a falsification-class check — on its diagnosis, not on its
   own estimator. Every task shipped a cheap decisive route that was never taken.
4. **Coherence checks computed inside a wrong frame reliably confirm it.** Sharpest instance: G24 `wVmAykK`
   compared its IPS estimate (0.305) with an on-policy anchor (0.2975) **at the same wrong grain** and called
   the agreement "astonishingly close… highly accurate". At the correct grain it is 0.305 vs 0.3846 and fails.
5. **Right decision, wrong science, in 6 of 13 failures.** On G24 all three trials got the launch call right
   on all four extracts while every policy value was outside tolerance: a decision-only grader would have
   scored the task 3/3 instead of 0/3.
6. **Difficulty tracks one design property.** Every task whose scientific object is *stated* in the workspace
   was solved; every task whose object must be *derived* was not — G34 the single exception, solved in ~20
   steps with zero warehouse queries.
7. **The failures are model-side.** Zero tool errors, timeouts, permission failures, OOM or import failures
   in any failed final-suite trial; all 13 reproduce from the agents' own submitted code.

**Corrected — and these corrections matter more than the original claims.**
- **F9 is not dominant.** The report claimed 10/13 failed trials were "right method family, wrong statistical
  object". Under a strict bar it is **4/13** (6 across the whole pool). The dominant labels are **F1**
  (committed to an identifying assumption, never tested it) and **F4** (correct diagnosis, semantically wrong
  repair), each with **F7** secondary.
- **A new label was needed: F11 coherence stopping** — a check is run, agreement obtained, and agreement
  treated as confirmation when the check had no discriminating power. This is what actually terminated most
  trials, and it is distinct from "no validation".
- **Two task-side defects exist, both outside the final five.** The Task02 explicit-invariant *ablation* is
  contradicted by its own graded reference (`tests/reference.py:73` does not select `synced_at` for support
  tickets) and its 0/3 is uninterpretable. G36's frozen grader used a household-weighted response against a
  load-weighted contract, so its "0/3" would be **2/3 under a correct grader**, with one genuine model failure.
  G36 remains excluded from every aggregate.
- **Four of the twelve pilot tasks are documentation-lookup exercises** (Task03, Task05, G35, G42) by the
  project's own admission test, and Task06 has two answer leaks.
- **A harness defect was found and disclosed** after the audit: the verifier process sweep could kill the
  container's own main command (`sleep infinity`, PID 7, starting 5.98 s after PID 1, outside a 3 s grace),
  producing reward 0 with empty verifier stdout. Signature: reward 0 + 0-byte verifier stdout + ~2 s verifier
  phase. This raised the confidence of the G05 `JnK5hsR` invalid-infrastructure adjudication from MEDIUM to
  HIGH, and is written up in `research/harness_process_sweep_defect.md`.

**Why this cannot yet support the general claim.** The finding came from tasks built by one author, measured
on one model family, n = 3 per task, and the "stated vs derived" property was named *after* the results were
seen. It is consistent with the project's preferred explanation **and with at least eight rivals** (§12).
Distinguishing them requires a prospective set frozen before any target-model contact, with predictions and
rivals written down first. That is what this phase designs.

## 5. SCIENTIFIC LITERATURE REVIEW

**Verdict on our working hypothesis: well supported in a narrow, conditional form, and not as a blanket
claim.** The defensible version: *absent an externally grounded signal, the mechanisms that would discriminate
good reasoning from bad — self-confidence, self-critique, step-level reward models, self-consistency — are
weakly to non-discriminative, and they degrade specifically on harder problems, out of distribution, on
self-generated content, and in domains without automatic verifiers.* Nearly every method that works imports
information from **outside the chain being evaluated**. That introspective-vs-externally-grounded distinction
is the sharpest framing the evidence carries. **[preprint]** marks work that is not peer-reviewed.

**The strongest external support — estimand ≠ arithmetic.**
- **CLadder** (Jin et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2312.04350): step-wise F1 of **~99 % for causal-graph
  extraction, ~99 % for arithmetic on the chosen estimand, and ~50 % for deriving the correct estimand.**
  Accuracy by rung: association 83.4 → intervention 67.5 → counterfactual 62.1. This is our dissociation,
  measured by someone else.
- **QRData** (arXiv:2402.17644): GPT-4 statistical 78.9 % vs **causal 46.8 %**; error taxonomy over 50+
  failures — knowledge recall 44 %, data analysis 34 %, equation formulation 16 %, **calculation 6 %**.
- Backdoor-adjustment probe (arXiv:2410.11385): **20–30 % for every model**, invariant to 0/1/2-shot, CoT and
  graph complexity, while factual inference on the same graphs reaches 83 %. Prompt- and
  complexity-insensitivity is the signature of *not running the procedure*.
- **StatQA** (NeurIPS 2024 D&B): *"LLMs primarily make applicability errors, whereas humans mostly make
  statistical task confusion errors"* — right method family, preconditions unchecked.
- **Corr2Cause** (ICLR 2024): 94.74 F1 → **61.73** under paraphrase, 67.87 under variable renaming — apparent
  competence can be shortcut learning.

**Self-correction without external feedback.**
- **Huang et al., ICLR 2024** (arXiv:2310.01798): intrinsic self-correction *degrades* — GPT-4 GSM8K
  **95.5 → 91.5 → 89.0**; GPT-3.5 CommonSenseQA **75.8 → 38.1**. With **oracle correctness labels**:
  75.9 → 84.3, 75.8 → 89.7. At matched compute, self-consistency@9 (88.2) beats multi-agent debate (83.0).
- **Kamoi et al., TACL 2024** 12:1417: *"no prior work demonstrates successful self-correction with feedback
  from prompted LLMs, except … tasks that are exceptionally suited"*; self-correction works where **reliable
  external feedback** exists. Documents oracle-label leakage in Reflexion and RCI.
- **Tyen et al., Findings of ACL 2024** (arXiv:2311.08516) — the sharpest localisation of the deficit: poor
  self-correction *"stems from LLMs' **inability to find logical mistakes**, rather than their ability to
  correct a known mistake"*, and they struggle *"even in highly objective, unambiguous cases"*. **A small
  out-of-domain classifier beats prompting a large model at detection.**
- Olausson et al., ICLR 2024: self-repair gains are *"modest, vary a lot … and are sometimes not present at
  all"* once cost is counted; better *external* feedback dominates.

**Does the model seek disconfirming evidence?**
- **Jhaveri et al., arXiv:2604.02485 [preprint]** — interactive Wason 2-4-6 plus a Blicket causal-discovery
  domain, 192 episodes, 11 models: **Incompatible-to-Compatible test ratio 0.14–0.72 — every value below 1.0
  means more confirming than disconfirming tests.** Lower confirmation bias correlates with higher discovery
  success; dual-goal prompts raise success **42 % → 56 %** and raise I:C in 18 of 22 model×condition cases.
- **Krishnamurthy et al., NeurIPS 2024** (arXiv:2403.15371): in-context bandit exploration — across 32 core
  prompt designs **all but one** showed suffix failure or uniform-like failure; the one success required
  **externally summarised** history.
- **BoxingGym** (arXiv:2501.01540): scores experiments by **expected information gain**; GPT-4o *"struggle[s]
  with both experimental design and model discovery"*, and an agent given a scientist's verbal theory
  substantially underperforms the scientist — the explanation does not carry the epistemic content the data did.
- **Geng et al., arXiv:2505.17968**: *"LLMs fail to extract information from observations, reaching a
  performance plateau that falls short of the ideal of Bayesian inference"*; prompting them to **intervene**
  helps. Failure modes named **overcomplication** and **overlooking**.
- **Laban et al., arXiv:2505.06120** — **200,000+ simulated conversations**, six tasks: **39 % average drop**
  single-turn → multi-turn. *"Models make assumptions in early turns and prematurely attempt to generate final
  solutions, on which they overly rely"*; *"when LLMs take a wrong turn … they get lost and do not recover."*
- **Riddell et al., FORGE 2026** (arXiv:2601.22208) — the closest existing work to a professional setting
  (cloud RCA): **anchoring bias** (*"fixates prematurely on one hypothesis and neglects exploration of
  alternatives"*) and **failure to update belief** are the strongest negative predictors of correctness (risk
  difference < −0.15). Caveat: small open-weight models, LLM-as-judge annotation.

**Why the output is nonetheless convincing.**
- **Turpin et al., NeurIPS 2023**: CoT explanations *"can systematically misrepresent the true reason for a
  model's prediction"*; accuracy drops up to **36 %** under biasing features the model never mentions.
- **Chen et al., arXiv:2505.05410 [preprint]**: models reach **reward > 0.99** while verbalising the injected
  hack in **under 2 %** of examples in 5 of 6 environments; faithfulness is **44 % lower relatively on GPQA
  than MMLU** — it degrades where difficulty rises.
- **Si, Hashimoto, Yang — the Ideation-Execution Gap** (arXiv:2506.20803 [preprint]): 43 expert researchers
  each spent **100+ hours** executing a randomly assigned idea. LLM ideas, rated **more novel and more
  exciting** before execution, *"decrease significantly more … on all evaluation metrics (novelty, excitement,
  effectiveness, and overall; p<0.05)"* after it. **Persuasiveness and empirical merit come apart, and only
  running the test reveals it.**
- **Farquhar et al., Nature 630:625 (2024)**: semantic entropy detects confabulation at AUROC ≈0.790 — **and
  explicitly "does not help when LLM outputs are systematically bad"**. A confidently held, internally
  consistent, systematically wrong analysis has *low* semantic entropy and is invisible to consistency-based
  checking. This is the sharpest published statement that self-consistency cannot substitute for a
  discriminating test.
- **Xiong et al., ICLR 2024**: verbalised confidence separates a model's own correct from incorrect answers at
  **AUROC 0.522–0.605** — near chance.
- **Recchia et al., arXiv:2507.19486 [preprint]**: participants *"become more confident in the system's answers
  after conducting online research, **even when those answers are incorrect**."*

**Three circulating numbers that are wrong or unlocatable — do not repeat them:** the GPT-4 technical report's
supposed "ECE 0.007 → 0.074" (the report contains no ECE values); "26.28 % of correct responses contain flawed
reasoning"; "25–28.5 % of audited code-RL tasks accept a wrong solution".

## 6. PRODUCTION FAILURE RESEARCH

Every candidate in §16 traces to a documented instance. Findings are given as paraphrase plus exact numbers;
sources that resisted automated fetch are named as **pointer only** and must not be quoted.

**Interference and marketplace spillovers.** LinkedIn Feed (WWW 2015): direct β **0.0486** vs spillover γ
**0.1252** — spillover ≈**2.6×** direct. LinkedIn BR-vs-CBR (KDD 2017): unit-randomized 0.2338 vs
cluster-randomized 0.8123, p = 0.048, **with pre-launch balance checks and the pre-treatment A/A both clean**.
eBay (EC '14): user-level **+0.74 %** revenue vs an auction-level coefficient **indistinguishable from zero**
— the user-level number **overstates by more than 2×**; and control users spend ~**1.3 % less** against a
treated rival. Facebook Stories (KDD 2021): user-randomized showed creator metrics **flat**; cluster
randomization revealed **−0.25 %** daily active creators — a complete false negative on the supply side.
Facebook Jobs: **+71.8 % vs +49.7 %**, and *an earlier independent user-randomized test had replicated the
biased number* — replication gave false assurance. Airbnb (Mgmt Sci): **32.60 % ± 12.93 %** of the Bernoulli
estimate is interference, and **the sign is not fixed** (+15.09 % in demand-constrained, −27.41 % in
supply-constrained markets). Lyft: a toy two-agent model where the true ATE is 33 % and the naive lift is
200 % — a **6×** overestimate, and the post explicitly pre-empts "small sample" as the diagnosis.

**The best "ran the right test, got the wrong answer" case.** Airbnb's *price-suggestion* meta-experiment ran
the same interference diagnostic at the same sample size as the fee experiment and returned a **null** (bias
**54.16 % ± 65.05 %**; a **3.45×** larger sample would be needed), because ITT dilution from poor host
compliance shrinks both the effect and the detectable bias. An analyst who runs the recommended test and
reports "no interference here" has followed best practice and is wrong — and nudges are the common case.

**Triggering and segmentation on treatment-affected conditions.** Bing deeplink (*A Dirty Dozen*, KDD 2017
§5.8): **both segments showed a statistically significant increase in Sessions/User — Bing's key metric —
while the union showed no significant change.** Truth: zero effect; the treatment shrank one segment.
Discriminating test: a **per-segment SRM test**, available everywhere and almost never run. LinkedIn
(arXiv:1808.00114): a **better** model triggered **fewer** users — *effect size and bias are positively
coupled*, so the bias is worst exactly when you most want to ship; ~10 % of triggered analyses carried an SRM.

**Sample ratio mismatch, real causes** (KDD 2019, 25 causes, ~**6 %** of Microsoft experiments): MSN's carousel
12→16 read as a **significant decrease** in engagement with a ready-made choice-overload story — the most
engaged treatment users crossed a bot-detection threshold and were deleted from analysis; corrected, **the
sign flipped** and the feature shipped. Skype's config refresh lost 30 % of treatment sessions. An MSN A/A
test at **49.9/50** — one bucket in 1000, invisible to inspection. **Rule 1: SRM in the triggered scorecard
but not the standard one ⇒ the trigger condition is wrong.** Metric-level SRM: MSN's new-tab change showed
**+8.32 % Page Load Time** for a one-line JS change because 7.8 % fewer homepage loads removed the *fast,
cached* back-button reloads.

**Simpson's paradox with printed numbers.** KDD 2009 §6 Table 1: Friday at 99/1 and Saturday at 50/50;
treatment wins both days (2.30 vs 2.02; 1.20 vs 1.00) yet pooled shows **1.20 % vs 1.68 %** — **−29 %
relative** when the effect is positive every day. **The arithmetic is correct and the pooled number
reconciles exactly against total conversions and total sessions**, so it survives every reconciliation check a
data team owns. Bing ads (JMLR 2013 §2.4): CTR 6.2 % vs 7.5 % overall reverses to 5.1 vs 4.8 and 18.1 vs 15.6
on splitting — and **acting on the corrected conclusion likely loses money**, because the improved click
model pushes second-position ads below reserve.

**Novelty, peeking and stopping rules.** Most *suspected* novelty and primacy effects are artifacts (KDD
2012/2014); a cumulative-effect chart from an **A/A** test invites "good feature with primacy" (day 1 has a
**67 %** chance of falling outside the final 95 % bound). Four platforms converge on peeking: Optimizely
**5–10×** FPR inflation at 10,000 samples, approaching 100 %; Netflix **70 %** of A/A simulations declare
significance at some point; Etsy **>50 %** after 500 observations; Spotify — **GST run past its planned maximum
n inflates the FPR**, so a correct sequential method used incorrectly is still invalid. And the gem
(Optimizely footnote 2): stopping at p<0.05 **and** requiring post-hoc power >0.8 is a lower-α rule that
**itself inflates the FPR** — *the safeguard a careful team adds is part of the error*. A published **337 %**
lift reported p = 0.009 and **observed power 97 %**; true pre-experiment power ≈**3 %**, false-positive risk
≈**63 %**.

**Ads incrementality.** eBay brand keywords (*Econometrica* 2015): **99.5 %** of forgone paid clicks recaptured
by organic; for non-brand, OLS gives ROI **>4,100 %** raw and **>1,400 %** with time and geographic controls
while the experiment gives **−63 %, CI [−124 %, −3 %]** — *adding controls makes the wrong answer look
robust*. Lewis–Rao–Reiley: exposed-vs-unexposed overstates by **~200×** even after controlling for past search
intensity; and the **precision heuristic** — the SD of purchases is ~10× the mean, a 25 %-ROI campaign implies
an expected R² of **0.0000054**, so *if an observational method claims higher precision than a feasible
experiment, be suspicious*. Gordon et al. (Mktg Sci 2019): in **half** of 15 Facebook RCTs the observational
estimate is off by a **factor of three**, and a match score summarising **thousands** of behavioural variables
does not rescue it.

**Off-policy evaluation.** Criteo (WSDM 2018) benchmarked against **39 real online A/B tests**: capped
importance sampling at cap=100 — *common practice* — achieves correlation **−0.15**, precision 0.28,
**false-negative rate 0.64**. The asymmetric cost is stated explicitly: a false positive costs one A/B test; a
false negative is a real improvement never tested. Bottou et al. validated counterfactual estimates against a
real **18 %** reserve-reduction bucket, and their **inner confidence intervals grow sharply once the target
policy leaves the explored range** — a computable honesty signal missing from most replay pipelines.

**Feedback loops.** Chaney et al. (RecSys 2018): evaluation on confounded held-out data is **biased toward
recommenders resembling the deployed one** — and held-out accuracy is the organisation's actual acceptance
gate. Perdomo et al. (ICML 2020): performativity *"surfaces as ordinary distribution shift, which teams handle
by retraining"*, so the dashboard looks benign. **Google Flu Trends** (Science 343): overshot by **>50 %** and
was too high in **100 of 108 weeks**; MAE 0.486 vs **0.311** for a lagged-CDC baseline. Mechanism: Google's own
product changes (suggested searches, candidate diagnoses) altered the input distribution. It was coherent for
years because it validated against a decade of CDC data — and was *"part flu detector, part winter detector"*.
The discriminating test was embarrassingly cheap: compare with a naive lagged baseline and test the errors for
autocorrelation.

**Feature-store point-in-time correctness.** Feast's own docs: a `created_timestamp_column` is used to
deduplicate *"but it is not otherwise filtered. This means a value that was backfilled or corrected **after**
an entity dataframe timestamp can still be returned for it"* — **the default is not safe**. Databricks' worked
example pairs a feature timestamped **8:52** with a label observed at **8:50**: the realistic leak is two
minutes, invisible to every aggregate QA check. Airbnb **Chronon** advertises *"guaranteed point-in-time
correctness"* and — the part that matters — **measures** online/offline consistency as mismatch, missing, extra
and smape rates by replaying the join against logged inference queries. Uber's *Taming the ML Firehose*
reports **"0 % mismatch on key features that previously experienced mismatch rates of over 10 %"**, caused by
`en-US` vs `en` and `jp_JA` vs `jp-JA` — both sides non-null, correctly typed, passing schema validation.
Google's **Rules of ML #29** (log and wait), **#31** (a joined table may change), #32, #33, #37. **And the
trap: TFDV-style distribution-skew checks do not catch a PIT violation**, because it can leave the marginal
distribution unchanged.

**Leakage magnitude, verified.** Kapoor & Narayanan, arXiv:2207.07048 — **17 fields, 329 affected papers, an
8-type taxonomy**. *"[L2] Model uses features that are not legitimate… The judgement of whether the use of a
given feature is legitimate … requires domain knowledge and can be highly problem specific."* *"We found
errors in 4 of the 12 papers—exactly the 4 papers that claimed superior performance of complex ML models over
baseline LR models"*, and *"the difference between the AUC of the complex ML models and LR models drops from
0.14 to 0.01"*. *"None of the models outperform a baseline model that predicts the outcome of the previous
year."* And the sentence that makes it the strongest item in the review: **"while none of these errors could
have been caught by reading the paper, model info sheets enable the detection of leakage in each case."**
**Correction: Kaufman, Rosset, Perlich & Stitelman (KDD 2011 Best Paper; TKDD 6(4) 2012) is closed access from
every route tried. Its abstract phrasing and the KDD Cup 2008 / INFORMS 2010 anecdotes could not be verified
and must not be reproduced.**

**Time semantics.** GA: **"Changing the time zone only affects data going forward, and is not applied
retroactively"**, and **"you may see a flat spot or a spike in your data"** — with no marker in the data at the
cutover. **"Google Ads reports conversions against the date/time of the click… Analytics uses the date/time of
the conversion itself"** — both columns are called "Conversions", and the gap systematically penalises
campaigns with long lag. GA4 BigQuery export updates daily tables *"for up to three days"* and **"Events that
arrive after that three-day window are not recorded"**; attribution credit can change *"for up to 12 days"*.
Mozilla: `submission_date` is the *recommended* field because **"Do not assume that the time reported by an
instance of Firefox desktop is correct"** — and it manufactures a weekend artifact, so **choosing the
defensible field is what makes the analysis wrong**. PostgreSQL: `+ interval '1 day'` ≠ `+ interval '24
hours'` across a DST boundary (12:00 → 12:00 vs 13:00). Airflow: the logical date *"denotes the start of the
data interval, not when the Dag is actually executed"*, and **timedelta schedules "do not adjust for daylight
savings time when scheduling subsequent runs"** while cron schedules do. Spark watermarks: *"the guarantee is
strict only in one direction"* — so a streaming daily total is legitimately nondeterministic and the
reconciliation gap **has no single correct value**. Existence proofs: Azure's 2012 leap-day outage (*"taking
the current date and adding one to its year"*) and Cloudflare's leap second (**"the belief that time cannot go
backwards"**).

**Semantic layer.** Uber uMetric: **6.53M vs 6.20M** shopping sessions, same city, same window — *"one of the
tools (Summary) used stale filters in their static query that did not capture the latest and complete list of
rider states"*. Both numbers are internally consistent and each reconciles within its own tool; 5 % is small
enough to be waved away. **Diff the filter predicates, not the totals.** UA vs GA4: sessions restart at
midnight in UA and **not** in GA4; UA counts one conversion per session, GA4 every instance — so conversion
rate per session moves in numerator *and* denominator, in opposite directions. **Looker symmetric aggregates —
the inverted-coherence case:** *"When symmetric_aggregates is on, aggregate functions return correct results,
even when joins result in a fanout"*, **and it is on by default**. So in the tool the number is right, and the
analyst's own hand-written SQL "verification" (223.44 instead of 124.84) is the wrong one — **the independent
replication is what fails to tie out, and the analyst will trust it over the tool.** Uber on exact counts:
*"HyperLogLog … introduces a 1-5 % error, unacceptable for metrics like MAU that underpin financial
reporting"* — a **deterministic, reproducible** wrongness that ties out against every query using the same
function. Wikimedia pageviews carry a dated definition changelog while **the table and column names never
changed**.

**Proxy validity.** Office Online's revenue proxy showed a **64 % reduction in clicks per user** because the
new page displayed the *price* — the treatment changed the coefficient linking surrogate to outcome. Google's
ads-blindness work: a **90-day** study captures only ~**65 %** of the long-run learning effect, hence a
multiplier of **2–3**, and it drove a **50 %** reduction in mobile search ad load. **And the counter-result:**
Bing found the opposite sign, with the published resolution turning on power and on whether differential
abandonment was measured (**under 25 %** of cookies survive a two-month study) — two competent teams, similar
designs, opposite conclusions.

**Instrumentation.** Skype's push-notification change moved **call-connection metrics** because the app stayed
awake long enough to flush a telemetry batch — *the tell was that client-logged metrics moved while
server-logged siblings did not*. And the best single "internally coherent" case in the corpus: over several
months in 2013 Bing migrated its CDN and **several independent teams reported key metrics improving** — a
large part was click-beacon fidelity; swapping beacons for redirect tracking cut click loss by **more than
60 %** in some browsers. No bug, no SRM, multiple teams agreeing, and a plausible causal story.

**Pointer only — do not quote:** Airbnb's *Experiments at Airbnb* and the **Minerva** series, Netflix's
interleaving and *Distributed Time Travel* posts, Airbnb's Zipline post, DoorDash's and Lyft's blogs (all 403;
`web.archive.org` blocked). **The widely-repeated Airbnb early-stopping anecdote is UNVERIFIED.**

## 8. WHAT THEY DO NOT COVER

1. **No benchmark hands the agent a confident, authoritative, *wrong* prior analysis it must detect and
   overrule.** Every artefact in every reviewed benchmark is correct by construction. AvalancheBench states
   *"No dashboards or misleading artifacts"*; DAB supplies a hints file; BIRD supplies Oracle Knowledge.
   Nearest prior art: BAITBENCH plants a shortcut but measures whether the agent *takes* it; LiveSQLBench's
   Business Rule Drift changes authoritative knowledge across releases.
2. **No benchmark frames the task as diagnosing and repairing a specific flawed prior *analysis* across
   stages.** BIRD-CRITIC repairs a *labelled-broken* query; ELT-Bench builds a pipeline; OpenRCA diagnoses a
   software incident.
3. **Almost nothing measures whether the agent obtains evidence that could refute its own interpretation** in
   a professional setting with a decision attached. Popper does it for hypothesis validation on biomedical
   tables; CausalGame gives it two rubric points and finds **5–7 %** uptake.
4. **"These data cannot answer that question; here is the experiment that would"** is scored in exactly one
   benchmark (CausalDS) and one non-benchmark system (Decision Lab), and in **no** data-agent benchmark.
5. **Decision consequence is absent.** No reviewed benchmark ties the analysis to a stated business decision
   rule with a threshold and grades the decision *alongside* the quantities.
6. **Leakage is treated as a benchmark defect rather than as the finding** (MLE-bench patches it).
7. **The gap the literature leaves, stated precisely:** *no benchmark instruments, within a single agent
   trajectory and relative to the interpretation the agent currently holds, whether the next action was one
   whose outcome could have overturned that interpretation.* Three corners exist separately — **Jhaveri et
   al.** has the right construct (I:C ratio) in toy domains; **BoxingGym** has the right formalism (EIG) but
   aimed at the true model's parameters rather than the agent's belief; **BLADE** has the right target
   (justifiable alternatives) but computes coverage **across independent runs**, and states verbatim that it
   *"does not evaluate an agent's ability to interpret the results"*.
8. **Target-leakage detection by LLM agents appears genuinely unstudied.** The "LLM + leakage" literature is
   entirely benchmark contamination and multi-agent privacy — two unrelated senses that pollute any search.
9. **No benchmark for Simpson's paradox, collider or selection bias at scale**, and abstention is barely
   scored anywhere.

## 9. FORENSICDS RESEARCH GAP

> **A prospective test of whether frontier data-science agents can tell "my analysis is internally coherent"
> from "my scientific interpretation is valid", in production incidents where several interpretations are
> initially defensible, a discriminating test is available and cheap, and a named decision rides on the
> answer.**

Four things make this a contribution rather than another analytics benchmark:

1. **The incident architecture.** An incumbent artefact produces a specific number; the workspace contains the
   reasoning that produced it, the stakeholders who disagree about it, and the evidence that can adjudicate.
   This is the shape of the work and it is not represented in the literature.
2. **Discovery/validation separation.** The 15 measured tasks are the discovery set; the prospective set is
   frozen before any target-model contact, with six pre-registered predictions and eight rival hypotheses
   carrying explicit discriminators. No reviewed benchmark separates these, and several refine tasks after
   observing model failures.
3. **Layered validation as a graded object.** L1 coherence / L2 robustness / L3 falsification are
   distinguished by construction; the wrong route is designed to pass L1 and often L2; the L3 route is present
   but never named. Two archetypes extend this: **defer-or-overturn** (P31) and **identifiability-verdict**
   (P32).
4. **Decision-linked grading of the quantities beneath the decision.** Justified empirically: 6 of 13 phase-1
   failures reached the right decision with wrong quantities, and on G24 a decision-only grader would have
   scored 3/3 instead of 0/3.

**Honest positioning.** This is **not** a claim to have measured a new capability. Phase 1 is a discovery set
and its own audit corrected its headline. It is a claim that the *design* occupies territory the surveyed
field leaves empty, and that the prospective protocol can distinguish our preferred explanation from eight
rivals. The nearest relatives — AvalancheBench (latent-world recovery), CausalReasoningBenchmark
(identification-spec grading), Popper (executed falsification), OpenRCA 2.0 (causal-path grading) and BLADE
(justifiable alternatives) — each cover one axis; the combination, in an organisational incident with a
decision attached, is unoccupied.

## 10. PROPOSED NARROW SLICE

> **Decision-grade analytics repair under competing interpretations.**
>
> An organisation already computes a number. A named decision with a stated rule rides on it. The number may
> be wrong — for a *scientific* reason rather than a crash — and the evidence available admits several
> defensible interpretations. The data scientist must reconstruct how the data came to exist, determine which
> evidence discriminates among the interpretations, obtain it, revise if it contradicts them, repair the
> pipeline, and make the decision defensibly.

The capability under test is one thing: **discriminating-evidence selection**. The professional instantiation
is deliberately broad — ~17 domains in the candidate pool — because a narrow capability measured in one domain
is indistinguishable from domain knowledge. **This definition makes no reference to any model's behaviour.**

**Admission test — a task is in the slice only if all hold.**
1. an **incumbent artefact** produces a specific number, and the reasoning that produced it is inspectable;
2. a **named decision with a stated rule and threshold** rides on that number;
3. the correct scientific object is **underdetermined by the workspace documents** — it must be derived from
   how the data came to exist, or from an optimisation no document solves;
4. **≥3 initially defensible interpretations**, at least one of which yields a coherent, plausible result that
   passes the organisation's normal checks;
5. **≥2 discriminating routes exist in the workspace**, neither named, each with a predicted outcome under
   each interpretation;
6. the answer is **deterministic given the extract**, and plausible wrong answers are separated from it by
   more than the reporting precision;
7. the **decision margin** is narrow enough that a wrong interpretation changes the decision on at least half
   the graded extracts;
8. **≥2 legitimate methods** reach the same graded quantities within tolerance.

**In scope.** Tabular and semi-structured systems of record (warehouses, event logs, ledgers, landing zones,
instrument logs, decision logs with policy versions); an incumbent pipeline in Python or SQL to be repaired
and re-run; heterogeneous evidence including artefacts that are **authoritative and wrong** *and* artefacts
that are **authoritative and right**; causal, statistical, operational-research and economic estimands;
deterministic latent truth with hidden regimes; a decision rule with a threshold; grading of the quantities
beneath the decision as well as the decision; and — as two required archetypes per world — one incident whose
published number is **correct** and one whose honest deliverable is **"not identifiable from these data; here
is the design that would be"**.

**Out of scope.** Leaderboard model training; unstructured or multimodal inputs as the primary object;
visualisation; open-ended "insight generation" needing an LLM judge; tasks whose correct object is stated in a
document the agent reads anyway; tasks whose decision margin is so wide that any moderate error preserves the
decision; tasks whose accepted answer family is narrower than the analysis they claim to test; and tasks where
the discriminating evidence is not present in the workspace.

**Why not narrower or broader.** Narrower ("estimand selection in causal inference") would exclude the two
difficulty axes the project has actually demonstrated — execution-hard optimisation (phase-1 G41, where two of
three trials named min-cost flow and implemented a greedy augmentation) and economic-object errors — and would
re-run CausalReasoningBenchmark and CausalDS. Broader ("enterprise data science") admits documentation-lookup
tasks: four of phase 1's twelve pilot tasks were exactly that, and three scored 3/3.

## 11. FAILURE HYPOTHESIS

**H1 (primary, prospective).**
> **Discriminating-test omission.** When a production incident admits ≥3 defensible interpretations and the
> workspace contains a cheap test whose outcome differs across them, a frontier data-science agent will commit
> to one interpretation early, validate it with checks computed *inside* that interpretation, and not run the
> discriminating test — and this, rather than an inability to execute the analysis, will be the proximate
> cause of most failures.

**Pre-registered predictions**, each measurable from a frozen trial without further model exposure:

| # | prediction | measurement | what would disconfirm it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | in ≥70 % of failed trials, no L3 test from the task's inventory was attempted | pre-registered per-task discriminating-test inventory vs trajectory | L3 attempts in most failures |
| **P2** | trials that run any L3 test pass at a materially higher rate | pass rate conditional on L3 attempt | no difference, or L3-attempting trials fail as often |
| **P3** | the first consequential error is at a **commitment** step (object, assumption, population, feasible set) in ≥60 % of failures | first-error classification against the task's declared commitment list | failures dominated by arithmetic/implementation |
| **P4** | ≥2 of every 5 failures pass ≥3 of the agent's own coherence checks | count of L1 checks passed in failed trials | failures are incoherent, not coherent |
| **P5** | the business decision is nevertheless correct in 20–50 % of failures where the margin permits | graded decision vs graded quantities | decisions fail whenever quantities fail |
| **P6** | agents almost never *revise* a committed interpretation after producing contradictory evidence themselves | count of revisions following a self-produced contradiction | frequent revision |

H1 is **not** "the model is bad at statistics". It is a claim about the epistemic loop: generation and
execution are competent, selection of discriminating evidence is not. External support for the narrow form is
strong (CLadder's ~50 % estimand derivation against ~99 % arithmetic; Tyen et al. localising the deficit to
*detection*; Jhaveri's I:C ratios all below 1.0; Riddell's anchoring and failure-to-update as the strongest
negative predictors in professional RCA). Support for a blanket form is not (§12).

## 12. ALTERNATIVE HYPOTHESES

Eight rivals, each with a discriminator. Phase 1 cannot distinguish them; the prospective set is designed so
that it can.

**A1 — Capability, not disposition.** Agents fail because they cannot execute the correct analysis even when
told which one it is. *Discriminator:* an object-disclosed condition on a frozen task. If pass rates stay low,
A1 beats H1. Phase-1 has only a confounded precedent (the Task02 ablation, contradicted by its own reference).
*External support:* CausalReasoningBenchmark's spec score of 34.1 % is itself a capability measure.

**A2 — Budget economics.** Agents stop because continuing costs tokens and time. *Discriminator:* a
large-budget condition plus measurement of budget used at stop. Phase-1 evidence is against but not decisive:
failing trials used 200–900 s of 5,400 s. *External support:* CORE-Bench found raising the cap $4→$10 moved
GPT-4o only 26 %→31 % and *"agents tended to remain stuck"* — against A2.

**A3 — Objective framing.** The instruction asks for a deliverable, so agents optimise the deliverable. Asked
for a *defence*, they would test. *Discriminator:* a paired condition requiring a falsification artefact as a
graded output. **The cheapest and most informative single experiment available to the project**, and a design
recommendation regardless of outcome.

**A4 — Route availability.** The discriminating test is not in fact cheap or findable; practitioners would
miss it too. *Discriminator:* an expert human baseline on 2–3 tasks, plus a documented expert solve path
recorded before any model run. *External support:* DiscoveryWorld's 11 practising scientists reached ~66 %
completion and ~55 % explanatory knowledge — human baselines are not flattering either.

**A5 — Grading artefact.** Falsification is invisible to the verifier, so the behaviour cannot pay off and P2
is unmeasurable. *Discriminator:* grade the diagnostic explicitly (A3's design). *External support:*
OpenRCA 2.0's 14.5 pp AnySvc-minus-Path-Reachability gap is precisely what outcome-only grading hides.

**A6 — Domain knowledge, not epistemic behaviour.** Failures are textbook-knowledge gaps (what a competing
risk is, what switchback carryover is). *Discriminator:* tasks whose object is *elementary* once identified but
whose identification requires a discriminating test — P22 and P24 are built for this.

**A7 — Single-model artefact.** The phenomenon is specific to one small model. *Discriminator:* run the frozen
set on ≥2 model families before any behavioural claim. *External support:* Gemini-2.5-Pro reached 73 %
discovery success at I:C 0.66 in Jhaveri et al., and reasoning models reach **87.9 F1** on ProcessBench against
56.5 for the best purpose-built PRM — the best models are markedly less biased.

**A8 — Instruction-induced anchoring.** Our incident framings name a suspect ("the vendor says…", "Ops
disagree…"), which may *cause* early commitment. *Discriminator:* a neutral-framing arm on one task.

**Evidence that would reframe H1 even if the predictions hold — must be reported.** Process supervision
demonstrably works (PRM **78.2** vs ORM 72.4, gap widening in N). RL installs self-correction (SCoRe
**+15.6 %** MATH), so "cannot self-correct" is about prompted behaviour, not architecture. GPT-4o beats
stats-background humans on StatQA (64.83 vs 53.45). Semantic entropy reaches **0.790** AUROC. And the scale
direction is unresolved: the generation–verification gap **grows** with pretraining FLOPs while faithfulness
**falls** with scale — name the tension rather than picking a side.

## 13. DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Twenty principles, each traced to evidence rather than intuition.

**From the benchmark review.**
- **DP1. Anchor every graded quantity to a declared latent world, not to a reference answer.**
  AvalancheBench's construction is what makes it possible to grade *why* an answer is right.
- **DP2. Grade the scientific object separately from the deliverable**, or the score measures formatting.
  DataSpace: **52 %** of best-model failures were answer materialisation. CausalReasoningBenchmark: strategy
  **79.2 %** vs full spec **34.1 %**. KramaBench: design 41.6 % vs implementation 19.8 %. Therefore make the
  estimand, population, grain, temporal state, denominator, feasible set and adjustment set **named,
  separately-graded outputs**.
- **DP3. ⚠️ The wrong-but-authoritative artefact must not always be wrong.** A suite where the published number
  is always wrong is gamed by always disagreeing, which measures contrarianism. **All five current ForensicDS
  tasks have a wrong published number — a structural bias.** Every world must contain authoritative artefacts
  that are correct and should be deferred to, plus at least one incident whose number is right (**P31**), with
  hidden extracts varying which side is correct. BAITBENCH shows the adjacent failure: **57.1 %** of runs
  exploit a planted shortcut and telling the model not to reduces it by **6.21 pp**.
- **DP4. Score detection-and-reporting of a defect, not merely avoidance.** MLE-bench documents real target
  leakage in its own corpus and treats it as a defect to patch while an agent exploiting it is rewarded.
  Invert: the leak is the finding.
- **DP5. Require a precommitted falsifying observation, and grade whether the test could actually refute the
  claim.** Popper's own audit: ineffective falsification design **28.1 %**, "test breaks implication"
  **17.2 %**, misinterpreted p-values **35.9 %**. "Ran a test" is not the measurement.
- **DP6. Make abstention and non-identifiability scoreable.** One reviewed benchmark scores abstention
  (CausalDS; frontier abstention accuracy 56–75 %). **P32** implements it, with hidden extracts where
  identification *is* available so "always abstain" fails.
- **DP7. Penalise merges and fluent wrongness.** SCIRIGOR: agent claims agree with faithful and unfaithful
  results at **91.8 % vs 91.0 %**. DataCross: GPT-4o scores higher on Logic (0.41) than Factuality (0.33). Any
  rubric with an "insight" axis and no faithfulness gate rewards well-argued nonsense.
- **DP8. Instrument where the first error entered.** LongDS-Bench's **47-point** early-to-late decline;
  Traverse's judges locate the first mistake in under a third of runs. Declare the commitment list per task
  *before* freeze so first-error attribution is pre-registered.
- **DP9. Audit the gold answers adversarially and report agreement.** Measured gold error rates: **BIRD
  Mini-Dev 52.8 %, Spider 2.0-Snow 62.8 %**; ELT-Bench-Verified found benchmark-attributable errors in
  **82.7 %** of failed transformation tasks and **9.85 points** of apparent progress from fixing the
  benchmark. BLADE's expert–expert agreement of **75–80 %** is the realistic ceiling for human-anchored
  judgement.
- **DP10. Prefer deterministic checks; distrust judges.** InfiAgent-DABench rejected LLM judging at 67 %
  human consistency; AvalancheBench flags judge bias on length, position and lexical overlap with a
  same-family judge; Terminal-Bench permits a judge only *"if it can be shown that the verifier essentially
  never makes a mistake"*.
- **DP11. Seal the environment and log tool calls.** CausalGame documents agents probing simulator APIs to
  recover hidden scenario identifiers for **+18.5 points** and **39 sessions** declaring success below
  threshold.

**From the production-failure review.**
- **DP12. Build from documented failure structures, not invented ones.** Every candidate cites a real
  instance — Walmart's SKU-rationalisation reversal; the Twente static-meter study (**+582 %/−30 %** reading
  errors, ≥750,000 households); high-sensitivity troponin moving MI incidence **18 %→22 %** with no outcome
  improvement; observation stays accounting for **~40 %** of the HRRP readmission reduction; MedPAC's ~16 %
  MA coding intensity; Milliman's **8.0 %** adverse one-year reserve development every year back to 2016;
  Southwest's **16,700** cancellations and **$140 M** settlement; the ORR's 2018 timetable inquiry;
  Fowlie–Greenstone–Wolfram's **2.5×** deemed-vs-realised gap; the NAO's **35 %** possibly-overstated savings;
  Lee–Padmanabhan–Whang's bullwhip.
- **DP13. Design the wrong route to pass the organisation's own controls.** The documented coherent-but-wrong
  cases all did: the **Epic Sepsis Model** (AUC 0.63 vs a vendor-reported 0.76–0.83, missing 67 % of cases,
  deployed at hundreds of sites); **JPMorgan's CIO VaR** change, approved through model governance and
  disclosed to the OCC, projected to cut VaR **44 %**, ahead of **$6.2 bn** of losses; **Ofqual 2020**, which
  reproduced national grade distributions *exactly* and failed at the unit of decision; **Target Canada**,
  where every system-to-system check passed and nothing compared system to shelf; **PHE's 65,536-row
  truncation**, producing a complete-looking file showing a plateau; **Google Flu Trends**. Add from this
  phase: the Bing deeplink double-segment result; the CDN-migration metric improvements; the KDD 2009 pooled
  Simpson's example that reconciles exactly; the Looker inverted-coherence case; and **Kapoor & Narayanan's
  four peer-reviewed papers whose errors "could [not] have been caught by reading the paper"**.
- **DP14. The discriminating test must be a thing practitioners actually run.** In every documented case one
  existed and was cheap: external validation on local patients; parallel-running old and new models;
  evaluating at the unit of the decision; counting shelves; reconciling row counts; comparing against a naive
  autoregressive baseline; bureau performance on *rejected* applicants; calendar-year diagonals of a loss
  triangle; a waiting-list cohort; POS variance ratios; retained reference parts; **a per-segment SRM test**;
  **replaying the feature join against serving logs**; **the randomised-swap stratum for position bias**.
- **DP15. Prefer mechanisms with an independent physical or contractual aggregate** — SCADA feeder peaks,
  network offtake, the general ledger, RMA records, payroll rules engines, customer goods-receipts. These make
  ground truth unarguable and give the verifier a second derivation.
- **DP16. Small-count metrics are a trap unless the noise is the point.** TRIR changes are **96–98 %** random
  variation; Backblaze requires **50,000 drive-days** before an AFR is reportable. Either give the task enough
  exposure, or make the *uncertainty* the graded object (P23).

**Carried from the phase-1 audit.**
- **DP17. The object must be derived, not stated** — the strongest phase-1 regularity (five stated-object
  tasks solved, four derived-object tasks not). Independently corroborated by Terminal-Bench's written spec:
  instructions must *"avoid turning the task into a guided tutorial"*, and difficulty *"should come from the
  problem itself"*; and by WorkArena's **42.7 % → 3.0 % → 0 %** and GDPval's Under-contextualized ablation.
- **DP18. The decision margin must be narrow enough that the wrong object changes the decision** — six
  phase-1 failures had the decision right.
- **DP19. Never grade a stylistic choice; accept every legitimate method; and if two defensible methods
  disagree materially, the task is not gradable** (the G10 mixing-family lesson).
- **DP20. Verify the discriminating-test inventory before build** — ≥2 routes, with their expected outcome
  under each hypothesis written down.

**Two further principles adopted this phase.**
- **DP21. Specify the ask and the output contract exhaustively; specify the method never.** These are
  different axes and conflating them turns a diagnosis benchmark into a tutorial. The counter-pressure is
  real: OSWorld-Verified moved *toward* specification (explicit formats, exact paths) because
  under-specification produced **false negatives**, and SWE-Bench+ found that solution leakage plus weak tests
  meant **67.72 %** of "resolved" instances did not truly fix the issue.
- **DP22. Assume the hedging exploit and design against it.** APEX-Agents v1.1 and Terminal-Bench's
  adversarial agent independently found *"generating all possible answers with the hope that tests might only
  check existence of a correct answer but not absence of wrong answers"*. Our numeric verifiers are immune —
  **the moment we add a findings artefact we inherit it.** Mitigation: score a hedged item **zero**, forbid
  hedging in the contract, and add a mutation that submits the correct cause alongside three distractors and
  must score 0. Bercovich (arXiv:2604.28093) measures **over 15 %** of tasks in popular terminal-agent
  benchmarks as reward-hackable.

## 14. WORLD-BASED ARCHITECTURE

A **world** is a persistent fictional organisation: one set of systems of record, one document estate, one cast
of teams, one calendar of real events (migrations, incidents, policy changes, reorganisations). An **incident**
is a dated situation inside that world. Incidents share the organisation, not a trick.

**Five rules that keep a world from collapsing into one puzzle.**
1. **Independent mechanisms.** No two incidents in a world may turn on the same scientific mechanism.
2. **Independent systems.** Each incident's discriminating evidence must come from a different subsystem than
   the last, so an agent cannot learn "always check table X".
3. **Shared but non-decisive context.** The world's org chart, calendar and definitions are reused; the
   *decisive* facts are local to each incident.
4. **Dated consistency.** A migration that happened in March is in March for every incident, and an incident
   set before it must not see it. This is what makes a world worth building: the point-in-time structure is
   reused, not re-invented.
5. **No cross-incident answer leakage.** An incident's resolution may not be documented anywhere an agent
   working a later incident can read.

**Calibration against the field** (§4b): APEX-Agents averages **166 files and 14.5 tasks per world**;
APEX-Accounting **16 tasks per world**; TheAgentCompany **175 tasks from one company**. ForensicDS today has
~25–31 files and **one task per world**. The field's world-construction method is either (i) let professionals
actually do the job for 5–10 days and keep the exhaust, or (ii) **write a per-world specification listing every
file, every task, and a trap register cataloguing every seeded contradiction** before any file exists. (ii) is
what we can execute, and it is adopted below.

## 15. 1,000-TASK SCALING MODEL

```
task = WORLD × MECHANISM × INCIDENT ARCHITECTURE × EVIDENCE CONFIGURATION
              × DECISION TYPE × DATA-GENERATING REGIME
```

| axis | cardinality | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **world** | 8–12 authored organisations | Meridian Retail · Halcyon Health · Lattice Financial · an industrial OEM · a freight network · an energy retailer · a marketplace · a B2B software company |
| **mechanism** | 30–40 | selective labels · informative censoring · interference · staggered adoption · measurement-system change · vintage/backfill · performative feedback · hierarchical reconciliation · wrong feasible set · non-linear economics · transport to a new population · multi-stage selection · competing risks · denominator/exposure · proxy-outcome validity · position bias · endogenous prices |
| **incident architecture** | 6–8 | a published number is disputed · two teams disagree · a model degraded in production · a gate decision is due · an external party challenges a certificate · a plan must be committed · a metric moved and nobody knows why · a regulator asks for a restatement |
| **evidence configuration** | 5–8 | warehouse + incumbent pipeline · warehouse + experiment registry + ops logs · landing zone + issued ledger + contract · model registry + decision logs + policy versions · instrument logs + calibration records + retained samples · finance semantic layer + source systems |
| **decision type** | 6–8 | launch/hold · procure/defer · price/hold · extend/stop a programme · certify/remediate · reserve/release capital · hire/overtime · restate/do not restate |
| **DGP regime** | 3–5 per task | effect present/absent/reversed · strong/weak censoring · heavy/light seasonality · tight/loose capacity |

Naive product ≈ 411,000. **That is not the claim.** The claim is that **~1,000 semantically distinct tasks** are
reachable because the binding constraint is *authored mechanism × world pairs*: ~10 worlds × 35 mechanisms ≈ 350
viable pairings, of which perhaps 250 survive admission, each yielding 3–5 genuinely distinct incidents by
varying architecture, evidence configuration and decision type. **The DGP regimes give hidden extracts, not new
tasks.**

**Anti-clone rule.** A variant is admitted only if it changes at least one of the following relative to every
existing task, and the change alters what the analyst must *think*, not merely what they must type:
(1) the **mechanism**; (2) the **object class** (population · grain · temporal state · denominator ·
counterfactual · action unit · feasible set · hierarchy · economic mapping · measurement scale); (3) the
**discriminating-test class** (negative control · placebo period · randomised stratum · dual-instrumented
period · retained reference sample · held-out vintage · natural experiment · independent system invariant ·
synthetic-censoring replay · on-policy anchor); (4) the **decision type**. **Two variants sharing mechanism
*and* object class *and* discriminating-test class are clones, however different their domain skin** — which is
why "marketplace interference × 20 domains" is one task, not twenty.

**Expert workflow.**

| stage | who | output | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. world authoring | 1 domain operator + 1 data engineer | systems of record, document estate, event calendar, generator skeleton, **trap register** | ~2–3 weeks per world, amortised over 20–80 tasks |
| 2. mechanism specification | methodologist | estimand, wrong objects, discriminating tests, identification argument | ~1 day per mechanism, reused across worlds |
| 3. incident authoring | domain operator + methodologist | instruction, documents, incumbent pipeline, decision rule and threshold | ~1–2 days per incident once the world exists |
| 4. pre-build gate | automated | separation of ≥10 wrong objects; margin test; discriminating-test inventory; identifiability by two independent routes | hours; **rejects roughly half of well-motivated designs** (phase-1 observed rate) |
| 5. verifier + mutation suite | engineer | graded quantities, tolerance calibration, 10–40 mutations, cheap-solve audit | ~1 day per incident |
| 6. adversarial review | independent reviewer | the single question "is the object stated anywhere?" plus an ambiguity audit **plus one adversarial exploit run** | ~2 hours per incident |
| 7. freeze and prospective run | automated | checksum, Oracle=1, Nop=0, then and only then the target model | ~$1–3 per task |

**Measured cost basis from phase 1:** engineering cost per task fell from ≈3,500 to ≈1,000 Python lines once the
harness was reusable, and target-model trials cost **$0.04–0.30** each. The scarce input is not compute; it is
**mechanism specification and adversarial review**, which is exactly why the world layer is worth building — it
amortises everything except stages 3, 5 and 6.

**Three field calibrations to respect.** TheAgentCompany measured **~17 person-hours per task**; PaperBench
*"many tens of hours"* per rubric and *"challenging to train others to create rubrics at our desired quality
level"*. Terminal-Bench's ~3 reviewer-hours per task still left **28 of 89** tasks defective, worth **12 points**
of understated capability. And every strong benchmark **shrinks**: APEX-Agents 480→240, CORE-Bench 90→39,
Terminal-Bench 80→89→74→66. **Task count is not the achievement.**

## 16. CANDIDATE INCIDENTS

36 candidates. Field key: **C** domain · **E** incident · **F** consequence/decision · **I** hidden scientific
issue · **J** object to derive · **L** the tempting sophisticated wrong route · **O** the falsification that
exposes it · **S** verifier strategy. (The full A–Z fields — including role, evidence inventory, hypothesis
list, applicable methods, why it looks coherent, coherence checks passed, independent validation, expected
chain, multiple-valid-method handling, cheap-solve risk, ambiguity risk, realism source, scaling family, novelty
and admission result — are in `research/phase2/candidates_01_10.md`, `_11_20.md`, `_21_30.md`, `_31_32.md`,
`_33_36.md`.)

### P01 — The promotion that paid for itself on paper
C: grocery retail, trade promotion ·
E: a 4-week multibuy on a coffee line shows +38 % unit uplift and the category team want it in the annual calendar; Finance sees category margin flat.
F: £14 m annual promo budget reallocation; the governance rule funds a mechanic only if incremental category margin ≥ £0.9 m.
I: incrementality must be measured on the **category and basket margin object**, not the promoted SKU's units, and over a window long enough to contain the post-promo dip.
J: incremental category contribution margin per promo pound, over promo plus recovery window, net of cannibalisation and forward-buy.
L: a careful matched-store DiD on the *promoted SKU's* units with store and week effects, correct standard errors, and a clean pre-trend on that outcome.
O: run the same estimator on **category** margin in the same stores, and extend the window past the promo: the uplift shrinks and a negative dip appears. A second route: the buyer's "new shopper" claim is falsified by loyalty-ID first-purchase counts.
S: grade incremental category margin, the cannibalisation and forward-buy components, the recovery-window length implied, and the funding decision; hidden extracts vary competitor activity and pantry-loading strength.

### P02 — The elasticity that came from the price changes we chose
C: grocery pricing ·
E: a log-log demand model estimated on two years of price moves says own-price elasticity is −1.9, so a 4 % price cut on 200 lines "pays back"; the trading director has seen this fail twice.
F: a £30 m revenue decision on 200 lines.
I: observational price variation was **policy-induced**; the only exogenous variation in the estimate sits in the forgotten randomised test.
J: own-price elasticity identified from exogenous variation, for the specific line group and format the decision applies to.
L: a sophisticated panel model: store and week fixed effects, promo flags, competitor price control, clustered SEs, log-log, with sensible diagnostics.
O: estimate elasticity on the randomised-test stores only: it is materially smaller. Equivalently, split the observational variation by **reason code** — competitor-match moves and cost pass-through moves imply different elasticities, which they cannot if the parameter is structural.
S: grade the exogenous elasticity, the observational-vs-exogenous gap, the segment-specific estimates, and the price decision.

### P03 — The shelf got better the week we changed the scanner
C: retail operations ·
E: on-shelf availability jumps from 94.1 % to 97.3 % in week 32, exactly when a new handheld gap-scan app shipped; the supplier penalty regime pays out on availability.
F: £2.2 m in supplier service-level penalties and a decision to stop a remediation programme.
I: the metric's **instrument** changed; historical and current availability are not the same measurement.
J: availability on a *single* consistent definition and sampling frame, over the whole period, and the penalty exposure implied.
L: a careful interrupted-time-series on the published availability series with a level-shift term at the app release, reporting a significant improvement net of seasonality.
O: the **dual-instrumented overlap**: on the same store-days the two apps disagree systematically; the "improvement" is a measurement bridge, not behaviour. A second route: availability reconstructed from inventory and sales shows no break.
S: grade the bridged series, the instrument effect, the behaviour effect and the penalty figure; hidden extracts vary overlap length and the direction of the instrument bias.

### P04 — Two forecasts, both reconciled, one buy plan
C: supply-chain planning ·
E: the SKU-level forecast sums to 4 % above the category forecast the commercial team signed; the planning system reconciles top-down and the buy is committed on the reconciled numbers, which are now 7 % below last year on a growing category.
F: a £40 m seasonal buy; over-buy risks markdown, under-buy risks lost sales.
I: reconciliation is a **statistical choice**, not bookkeeping: top-down, bottom-up and MinT imply different variances and different buys, and the correct one depends on where the signal is.
J: a coherent forecast whose reconciliation is justified by measured level-wise accuracy, evaluated on the vintage that would have been available at buy time.
L: a clean MinT implementation with the covariance estimated on the *revised* actuals and the *current* vintage, reported as "statistically optimal reconciliation".
O: a **held-out vintage backtest**: rebuild the forecast as of last season's buy date using only then-available actuals, and score all three reconciliations against the outcome. The current-vintage advantage disappears.
S: grade level-wise accuracy on the held-out vintage, the chosen reconciliation's weights, the resulting buy, and the markdown/lost-sales exposure.

### P05 — The supplier we were about to delist
C: procurement / supplier quality ·
E: supplier B's defect rate has doubled to 1.8 % while supplier A sits at 0.6 %; the delist threshold is 1.5 %.
F: delisting a supplier worth £18 m of annual volume and a 6-week transition risk.
I: the inspection data are a **multi-stage non-random sample** whose selection probabilities are known but not applied; the naive rate estimates the wrong quantity.
J: the design-weighted defect rate per supplier per period, on a consistent defect definition, with the contractual population.
L: a careful logistic model of defect probability with supplier, month, SKU and plant effects, reporting an adjusted odds ratio for supplier B — "controlling for mix".
O: the **random audit stratum**: in it, B's rate is barely above A's. Conditioning on inspection cannot fix selection *into* inspection.
S: grade weighted rates per supplier, the taxonomy-adjusted series, the audit-stratum estimate and the delist decision.

### P06 — The cohort that stopped shrinking when we merged the cards
C: retail CRM / loyalty ·
E: 12-month cohort retention improved 4.8 points for cohorts after March, which is when a household-identity merge shipped; the CRM team want to claim the improvement for their onboarding programme.
F: a £6 m CRM budget reallocation and a target set on retention.
I: the unit of analysis is an **identity whose definition changed mid-series**, and the merge is *not* random with respect to activity — active households are more likely to be merged.
J: retention on a consistent identity spine, with cohorts defined by the identity in force at acquisition, and merge-induced survivorship removed.
L: a survival model with cohort and tenure effects and a March indicator, concluding the hazard fell after March net of composition.
O: a **known-answer subset**: households the merge register shows were never merged show no improvement. Second route: reconstruct pre-March cohorts under the *post-March* identity rule — the historical improvement disappears.
S: grade retention on the consistent spine, the merge-induced component, and the budget decision.

### P07 — Margin per order, once the kitchen is already paid for
C: rapid-delivery unit economics ·
E: the 40-site rapid-delivery arm reports −£0.42 contribution per order on fully-allocated costs; the exec proposal is to close the 12 worst sites.
F: closing 12 sites, £9 m of annualised cost and a strategic retreat.
I: the decision needs the **incremental** cost and revenue of the marginal order and of a site's closure, not an allocated average; and the courier tier makes the cost curve kinked.
J: contribution margin under the relevant counterfactual: marginal order at current volume, and site closure net of demand displacement.
L: a careful site-level regression of cost on volume with site fixed effects, yielding a marginal cost per order that is then compared with revenue — reported as "the true incremental economics".
O: the **kink**: estimate the cost curve separately either side of the courier tier boundary — the pooled slope is a weighted average of two regimes and misprices exactly the sites near the boundary. Second route: the closed sites' catchments show demand recapture in stores, which the closure case ignores.
S: grade incremental margin per order at each site, the tier-boundary effect, the displacement rate, and the closure list.

### P08 — The dispatch change that worked in every window
C: delivery marketplace ·
E: a switchback test of a new dispatch policy shows +2.1 % completion rate and the team want a full launch; ops report couriers "chasing" the new batching behaviour across window boundaries.
F: a platform-wide dispatch change affecting 40 k couriers.
I: switchback validity depends on **carryover relative to window length**; the state that carries over is courier position, and the estimator must either use a washout or model the carryover.
J: the policy effect under a design whose identifying assumption (no carryover beyond the washout) is *tested*, at the decision's time-of-day mix.
L: a careful mixed model on 30-minute windows with city and time-of-day effects and cluster-robust SEs — a textbook switchback analysis.
O: estimate the effect as a function of **minutes since the last switch**: it ramps, which is carryover, and the first-minutes estimate is contaminated by the previous policy. Second route: compare with the 2-hour-window pilot — a longer window implies a larger effect if carryover is present.
S: grade the washout-based effect, the carryover profile, the window-length comparison, and the launch decision.

### P09 — What the earnings guarantee will cost once couriers know about it
C: gig economy / workforce incentives ·
E: a pilot guaranteeing £14/hour in three cities cost £0.31 per delivered order; scaling nationally is budgeted at £4.2 m on that basis.
F: a £4.2 m commitment that the finance committee will hold the team to.
I: the treatment **changes the data-generating process it is evaluated on**: the cost depends on a behavioural response that grows with awareness, so a 6-week pilot underestimates the steady state.
J: steady-state cost per order under full awareness and the national supply/demand mix — an extrapolation whose *dynamics* must be estimated, not an average.
L: a careful matched-city synthetic control on cost per order with pre-period fit and placebo cities, reporting £0.31 with a tight interval.
O: plot cost per order **against weeks since announcement**: it is still rising at week 6. Second route: the second wave, with a longer announcement lag, shows a different trajectory at the same tenure — inconsistent with a static effect.
S: grade the steady-state cost, the trajectory parameters, the transported national figure, and the budget decision.

### P10 — The media model and the geo test disagree
C: marketing measurement ·
E: the MMM says paid social ROAS is 3.1 and the CFO wants to shift £12 m into it; a geo holdout run last quarter implies 1.4.
F: a £12 m budget shift.
I: the two numbers are **different objects**: geo-lift measures incremental short-run sales in test markets; the MMM coefficient measures a modelled long-run response, on a *different conversion source*. Reconciliation requires making them comparable, not choosing a winner.
J: an incrementality estimate on a stated horizon and conversion source, with the MMM recalibrated to the experimental result.
L: a rigorous MMM rebuild: better adstock, saturation curves, cross-validation, VIF checks — concluding 3.1 is robust and the geo test was noisy.
O: a **placebo geo test in a pre-period** (markets that were never treated) shows the geo estimator is unbiased, so its narrower interpretation stands; and re-running the MMM on **CRM conversions** rather than platform conversions collapses the coefficient — identifying double counting as the mechanism.
S: grade the calibrated ROAS on the stated horizon, the double-counting magnitude, the placebo result, and the budget decision.

### P11 — Interleaving says ship, the A/B says no
C: search / ranking ·
E: an interleaving experiment prefers ranker R7 in 61 % of impressions; the subsequent A/B shows sessions-per-user down 0.4 %. Launch review is Friday.
F: shipping or shelving a quarter of ranking work.
I: the two experiments estimate **different objects under different bias models**, and the click-propensity model that interleaving relies on is invalid after the UI change.
J: the session-level utility effect, plus a corrected relative-preference estimate with re-estimated position bias — and an explicit statement of which object the launch rule is written on.
L: a rigorous interleaving analysis with per-user clustering, sequential-test correction and sensitivity to tie-breaking — concluding the preference is real and highly significant.
O: the **randomised-swap stratum** reveals the position-bias curve changed; re-weighting removes most of the preference. Second route: decompose clicks into "click then return to results" vs "click then end session" — R7's gain is concentrated in the former, which the session metric penalises.
S: grade the debiased preference, the position-bias parameters, the session-level effect and the launch call.

### P12 — Engagement is up, nothing downstream moved
C: recommendations / product analytics ·
E: a new "because you watched" carousel lifts engagement (items started per session +7.2 %, p < 0.001) but 8-week retention is flat; the launch gate is written on retention and the team propose shipping on the engagement proxy "since retention is underpowered".
F: shipping a surface that may be neutral or negative for retention, and setting a precedent for proxy-based gates.
I: **surrogate validity is an empirical property that can be tested on the platform's own launch history**, and it is conditional on the mechanism of the change.
J: the effect on the gate metric, plus a defensible statement of surrogate validity for *this class of change* — i.e. the predictive relationship estimated on comparable historical launches.
L: a careful surrogate-index analysis: regress long-term retention on short-term engagement across historical experiments, get a strong relationship, and translate +7.2 % engagement into a predicted +0.9 % retention with a confidence interval.
O: **split the historical launches by mechanism**: for changes that alter *content mix* rather than *content volume*, the surrogate relationship is flat or negative. ## 20. TOP 6 PROSPECTIVE VALIDATION TASKS

Selected on the scorecard, then filtered by the diversity constraint. **P23 (55) would otherwise be in this six;
it is displaced by the diversity constraint** — it shares the manufacturing domain and the inference-validity
mechanism cluster with P22 — and becomes the first task of the second wave, with P12.

| # | task | domain | mechanism | decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **P27** roster that is optimal and illegal | workforce ops / freight logistics | wrong feasible set + stochastic feasibility | headcount vs overtime |
| 2 | **P20** no-show model degraded by its own deployment | healthcare operations | policy feedback on the evaluation population | retrain / replace / change policy |
| 3 | **P13** limit increase that looks safe on approved accounts | consumer credit | multi-stage selection (selective labels) + performativity | programme go/no-go |
| 4 | **P22** yield drop after a gauge recalibration | discrete manufacturing | measurement-system change | supplier change vs spec recalibration |
| 5 | **P02** elasticity from the price changes we chose | grocery pricing | policy-induced (endogenous) variation | price change on 200 lines |
| 6 | **P07** margin per order once the kitchen is paid for | rapid-delivery unit economics | economic object + non-linear cost | close 12 sites or not |

**P27.** An optimiser says the depot covers next quarter's volume with 12 fewer drivers, saving £1.1 m; the
transport manager says the roster cannot be worked. The optimisation is mathematically correct over the **wrong
feasible set**, and the decision also needs a **stochastic** feasibility notion — a roster must be workable on a
bad day. Object: minimum sustainable headcount such that a compliant roster exists at a stated service level
under demand and absence variability, plus the cost-optimal headcount/overtime mix. Six evidence systems
(optimiser, contracts, certification register, payroll rules engine, absence history, forecast).

**P20.** A no-show model driving reminder calls has seen AUC fall 0.78 → 0.66 in six months; the vendor blames
drift and proposes retraining. The evaluation population *and* the outcome are endogenous to deployment, so
measured AUC conflates model degradation with **policy success**. Object: discrimination on a policy-invariant
population, the causal effect of reminders, the backfill component, and the retrain decision. The naive action
(retrain) is the wrong action, so a lucky decision is unlikely.

**P13.** A model says raising limits for low-risk accounts adds £8 m revenue at +0.3 pp loss; policy requires the
loss estimate to be defensible for the whole eligible population. Two stacked issues: **selective labels** (who
has an outcome) and **performativity** (the treatment changes the outcome). Object: expected loss under the
proposed policy for the eligible population, incorporating the limit's causal effect on default.

**P22.** First-pass yield dropped 97.2 % → 92.8 % in week 19; scrap is up £180 k/month and the bar supplier is
blamed. The pass/fail outcome is produced by an **instrument whose bias changed** while the specification did
not. Object: capability and true nonconforming rate on a single measurement reference, with the measurement-bias
component quantified and the material effect estimated after removing it.

**P02.** A log-log demand model on two years of price moves says elasticity is −1.9, so a 4 % cut on 200 lines
"pays back"; the trading director has seen this fail twice. The observational variation was **policy-induced**;
the only exogenous variation sits in a forgotten randomised test. Object: elasticity identified from exogenous
variation, for the specific line group and format the decision covers.

**P07.** The 40-site rapid-delivery arm reports −£0.42 contribution per order on fully-allocated costs; the exec
proposal closes the 12 worst sites. The decision needs the **incremental** cost and revenue of the marginal
order and of a closure, and the courier tier makes the cost curve **kinked**. Object: contribution margin under
the relevant counterfactual, and closure net of demand displacement.

## 26. VERIFIER FEASIBILITY

Common shape, inherited from the phase-1 harness: the agent leaves a runnable command; the verifier re-runs it
**unprivileged** (`setpriv --reuid=65534`) on the visible extract **and three hidden extracts** regenerated by
the shipped generator; reward is binary on exact bookkeeping plus the scientific quantities within a calibrated
tolerance plus the decision. `/tests` is mode 700, wheels are hash-pinned, and `runtime_manifest.sha256` guards
the image.

| task | graded quantities | tolerance basis | why it does not overfit to one method |
|---|---|---|---|
| P27 | minimum sustainable headcount; the set of binding constraints; simulated service level; cost-optimal headcount/overtime split | headcount and binding-constraint set **exact**; service level to 0.1 pp from a fixed-seed simulation specified in the contract | any solver or formulation reproducing the same feasible headcount and cost passes |
| P20 | holdback AUC and lift; the treated-population decomposition; the backfill component; the retrain decision | AUC to ±0.01 from a Monte-Carlo reference; decomposition shares to ±2 pp | holdback evaluation, a policy-aware correction or an IPW reconstruction all accepted |
| P13 | PD on the eligible population; the limit dose-response slope; programme loss; go/no-go | PD to ±0.3 pp (**3× the stratum's sampling SE, pre-registered**); slope sign and magnitude band | the random stratum or any assumption-checked reject inference that reproduces it |
| P22 | measurement bias (µm); corrected nonconforming rate; residual lot effect; supplier decision | bias to ±1 µm; rate to ±0.3 pp | reference-part bridge or second-machine comparison |
| P02 | exogenous elasticity; the observational-exogenous gap; segment elasticities; price decision | elasticity to ±0.15 (calibrated from the test's SE) | randomised-test estimate or a valid IV landing in the band |
| P07 | incremental margin per order per site; tier-boundary effect; displacement rate; the closure list | margin to ±£0.03; displacement to ±3 pp; **list exact** | natural-experiment or engineering-build routes |

**Two grading rules carried from the audit.** (1) **Grade the quantities beneath the decision** — 6 of 13
phase-1 failures had the decision right. (2) **Never grade a stylistic choice** — only quantities a
scientifically correct analysis must reproduce.

**Three harness capabilities to adopt that we are not currently using** (all native, none a fork):
- **Harbor multi-step tasks and `reward.json`** — per-step `tests/`, `min_reward` early stopping,
  `multi_step_reward_strategy`, and multi-dimensional rewards beside scalar `reward.txt`. This makes
  criterion-level scoring and staged verification (reconcile → localise → diagnose → repair → validate) a
  configuration choice. It answers DP2 and DP8 directly, and turns our binary reward into a **Mean Criteria@3**
  metric without weakening the binary headline.
- **The separate verifier container (TB 3.0+)** — artifacts are downloaded from the agent container and uploaded
  to a fresh verifier, which *"prevents many reward hacking vectors allowing for re-grading when task verifiers
  are updated"*. The re-grading half is the underrated one: it is what would let us fix a verifier defect like
  G36's **without re-running baselines**, and it retires the standing "verifier runs the agent's pipeline in the
  same container" weakness.
- **An adversarial exploit agent as a standing gate**, prompted as a cheating security researcher, with
  trajectories inspected **regardless of pass/fail**. Our static 21–38-case mutation suites cannot find *novel*
  exploits; a live run can.

**One instrument worth adding for credibility at zero model spend:** score ForensicDS against the **Agentic
Benchmark Checklist** (Zhu et al., arXiv:2507.02825). Terminal-Bench self-reports **0.896** average with two
admitted failures — *"Test case quality is not assessed via objective, automated metrics"* and *"Some
non-determinism remains"* — both of which our Monte-Carlo tolerance calibration and deterministic generators
address better than TB does.

## 28. AMBIGUITY AUDIT

| task | ambiguity risk | mitigation (in the workspace, decided before build) |
|---|---|---|
| P27 | which service level is committed; whether agency labour counts | the customer commitment and the labour policy state both; the rules engine is authoritative on compliance |
| P20 | which population the monitoring metric is defined on | the model-risk standard states monitoring must be on a policy-invariant population **without** saying the holdback is it |
| P13 | the eligible population; the horizon of the loss estimate | credit policy defines eligibility; the committee paper states horizon and risk appetite |
| P22 | which measurement is the specification reference | the drawing and the calibration procedure state the reference; the certificate dates the change |
| P02 | which line group and which horizon the price decision covers | the pricing policy names the group and the evaluation horizon |
| P07 | allocated vs incremental; whether displacement counts | the allocation-basis document is explicit, and the investment policy states closure cases must be net of recapture |

**The governing rule: the documents fix the *question* precisely and the *answer* not at all** (DP21). Two
independent readers must derive the same estimand from the documents alone before freeze.

**Three practices adopted from the field.** (1) Terminal-Bench's **two-verifier test**: *"if two reasonable
people read the problem description, a well-specified problem should result in both of them writing verification
algorithms so that anyone who passed one of the verifiers would also pass the other."* (2) **Overspecification
is audited as a defect too** (τ²-bench reviews for *"underspecification, overspecification, or non-unique
solutions"*). (3) **Measure specificity as a graded variable** rather than asserting it — GDPval had occupational
experts rate every prompt, and reports **89.07 % well-specified** as a benchmark property.

**The honest caveat.** Pre-release auditing is necessary and empirically insufficient: SWE-bench Verified found
**38.3 %** of samples underspecified out of 1,699; Terminal-Bench's ~3 reviewer-hours per task still left **28 of
89** defective, worth **12 points**; and τ-bench needed a third party to find its ceiling effects two years
later. Budget maintenance at the same order as authoring.

## 29. PROSPECTIVE-VALIDATION PROTOCOL

The discovery set is spent. The prospective set must be protected from the model it will be used to measure.

**Four governing rules.**
- **R1. No target-model exposure during design.** No "quick test to see if it is hard". **A task that has been
  shown to any target model is a discovery-set task for ever.**
- **R2. Expert solve path recorded first.** Before freeze, the author writes the intended solution *and* the
  intended discriminating test as a dated document, plus the sophisticated wrong route and its coherence checks.
  P1 and P3 are measured against this, so it cannot be written afterwards.
- **R3. Gates pass before spend.** The full list below, all model-free.
- **R4. Analysis plan pre-registered.** How trials will be labelled, and what each prediction's disconfirming
  result looks like, written before the first trial.

**Gate list (all before any target-model call).**

| gate | what it checks | fail action |
|---|---|---|
| scientific specification frozen | estimand, population, grain, temporal state, decision rule, tolerance basis | rewrite before build |
| workspace frozen + checksum | tracked-content hash recorded (`lock.json` → `task.digest`, not the deprecated dirhash); generator copies identical | re-freeze |
| **Oracle = 1** | the reference solution passes on the visible and all hidden extracts | fix task |
| **Nop = 0** | the untouched incumbent fails | fix task |
| wrong-analysis mutation suite | ≥10, ideally 20–40, scientifically plausible wrong solutions each scored 0; ≥1 behaviour-preserving mutant scored 1; **plus a hedging mutant that submits the right cause with three distractors and must score 0** | fix verifier |
| **separation margin** | every wrong object differs from truth by more than the graded tolerance on ≥3 of 4 extracts | reject design |
| **decision-margin test** | the wrong objects flip the decision on ≥half the extracts | narrow the threshold or reject |
| **discriminating-test inventory** | ≥2 named L3 routes present in the workspace, with expected outcome under each hypothesis | reject design |
| cheap-solve audit | no single filter/join/formula/doc-lookup/decision-guess reaches the graded answer — **each shortcut implemented and run** | fix design |
| **stated-object audit** | an independent reviewer searches the workspace for any sentence stating the object; if one exists, remove it or reject | mandatory |
| ambiguity audit | two independent readers derive the same estimand from the documents alone | rewrite docs |
| multiple-valid-method audit | ≥2 legitimate routes implemented and both pass; each accepted method recorded | widen the verifier |
| correct-table / identifiability test | two independent computational derivations of every graded quantity agree exactly | fix generator |
| hidden-extract validation | 3 regimes, decisions not constant, no extract within tolerance of a threshold | re-parameterise |
| grading evidence map | each graded quantity mapped to the reasoning step it tests, and to the mutation that would break it | complete before freeze |
| **adversarial exploit run** (new) | a cheating-prompted agent run, trajectory inspected regardless of pass/fail | fix verifier or environment |
| **resource-calibration audit** (new) | the per-task time, memory and step limits are shown not to bind for a competent solution | raise the limit |
| secret scan + leakage scan | no credentials; generator, scenarios and truth absent from the shipped image | fix build |

**Exposure procedure.**
1. Freeze; record checksum and commit; archive the expert solve path and the analysis plan.
2. Run Oracle and Nop on the frozen artefact; record rewards and digests.
3. Run **exactly three** target-model trials per task, sequentially, one trial per job, all on one task digest;
   do not adjudicate between trials.
4. If a trial is infrastructure-invalid (empty verifier output, ~2 s verifier phase, container teardown — the
   signature documented in `research/harness_process_sweep_defect.md`), adjudicate it **before looking at its
   reasoning**, and replace it with one authorised re-run.
5. Label trials against the pre-registered analysis plan. **No task change is permitted after step 3**, for any
   reason short of a demonstrated verifier defect — and a verifier defect **excludes the task from aggregates
   rather than licensing a revision** (the G36 precedent).
6. Run ≥2 model families before making any behavioural claim (rival A7).
7. Report **pass@1, pass@k and pass^k** from the same spend. Every persistent-world benchmark that measured
   reliability found it collapses (τ-bench pass^1 >60 % → pass^8 <25 %; APEX-Agents Pass^8 0.3–13.4 %;
   APEX-Accounting 2.6 %), and at $0.04–0.30 per trial this is affordable.

**The held-out set — the mechanism is solved, adopt it.** SWE-Lancer holds out tasks in which engineers
*"reintroduce the bug or issue in a distinct but equivalent way"*. For us this is nearly free: the generators are
parameterised, so a held-out task is **the same mechanism with a different world skin, data regime and trap
instantiation**, built after the discovery set is frozen and never piloted. **This is strictly stronger than
APEX-Accounting's "hold out the 10 hardest worlds", because it does not select on difficulty at all** — and it
directly answers the selection bias we already publish ("the suite was chosen with the same trials, so its pass@3
is biased low"), which APEX-Accounting names in identical terms. Corollary, stated by Terminal-Bench:
**once `candidates/` is public, every number from it is a development number; only an unreleased variant can
carry a prospective claim.**

**Two conditions worth pre-planning (not required for the main result).**
- **A3 condition (falsification-as-deliverable).** On 2 of the 6 tasks, a paired variant whose output contract
  additionally requires a named diagnostic (a pre-trend table, a placebo estimate, a design-weighted stratum
  estimate). Graded. This tests whether the behaviour is absent or merely unrequested, and it is the single most
  informative cheap experiment the project can run. **Build note: the moment this exists, DP22's hedging exploit
  is live** — score hedged items zero and add the hedging mutant.
- **A1 condition (object disclosed).** On 1 task, a variant whose instruction states the correct object but not
  how to compute it. Must be built as a **separate frozen task with its own verifier** so the phase-1 confound
  (an added paragraph contradicting the reference) cannot recur.

## 30. TRAINING-DATA IMPLICATION

**Exploratory. No result is claimed and nothing has been trained.**

If H1 survives prospective validation, the artefact the project produces is unusual: a **labelled epistemic
trajectory** rather than a problem–answer pair. Each frozen task can emit

```
evidence → hypotheses entertained → the object committed to → the checks run (L1/L2/L3)
        → the discriminating test available → whether it was run → the contradiction encountered
        → whether the interpretation was revised → the corrected analysis → independent validation
        → the decision
```

with the *correct* path known because the world is simulated, and the *taken* path recorded because the
trajectory is logged. Two possibilities this could support:

1. **Process-level supervision for an epistemic behaviour rather than an arithmetic one.** Existing
   process-reward work supervises steps of a derivation; here the supervisable step is *"did you obtain evidence
   that could have refuted you"*. There is reason for optimism — process supervision measurably works (PRM
   **78.2** vs ORM 72.4, with the gap widening in N) and RL has installed self-correction (SCoRe **+15.6 %**
   MATH) — and reason for caution: Tyen et al. found a **small out-of-domain classifier beats prompting a large
   model** at error detection, so the supervisable signal may be easier to learn externally than to install.
   Whether a reward model can learn *discriminating-test selection* is an open question.
2. **Counterfactual pairs at the commitment point.** Because the world is a generator, the same incident can be
   emitted with the discriminating evidence present or absent, and with the wrong interpretation true or false.
   That yields matched pairs in which the *only* difference is whether testing would have paid — the natural
   signal for **"test when it discriminates"**, as opposed to "always test", which is a cost. Jhaveri et al.
   provide the closest evidence that this is learnable at all: dual-goal prompting alone lifted discovery
   **42 % → 56 %** and raised the disconfirming-test ratio in 18 of 22 conditions.

**What we must not claim.** We have not trained anything, have not shown that failure-aware trajectories improve
any model, and have no evidence that supervising this behaviour generalises. **The honest statement is that the
benchmark produces the *kind* of data such an experiment would need, and that the experiment is not part of this
project.**

## 32. MERCOR / APEX RELEVANCE

**Separate notes only. No changes to ForensicDS, no Mercor branding in the benchmark, and the Abundant
assessment is not optimised around Mercor.** Recorded because the research is useful for a later, separate
proposal.

**Could professional data science be represented as shared enterprise worlds?** Yes, and the evidence is that
the strongest professional benchmarks already do it: APEX-Agents (33 worlds, mean **166 files**, **14.5 tasks
per world**), APEX-Accounting (10 worlds × **16 tasks**, each *"a self-contained company frozen at month-end
close"*), TheAgentCompany (**175 tasks from one company**). ForensicDS today is at ~25–31 files and **one task
per world** — roughly 1/14 the yield. The two transferable mechanics are **(i)** letting practitioners actually
do the job and keeping the exhaust, and **(ii)** APEX-Accounting's **trap register**: a per-world specification
listing *"every file, every task to be built (including the deliberate traps each is meant to catch), and a trap
register cataloging every seeded contradiction."*

**Could one enterprise world yield many scientifically distinct tasks?** Yes — but only under an anti-clone rule
stronger than domain skin. §15's rule (mechanism × object class × discriminating-test class) is the discipline
that keeps 16 incidents in one world from being one incident sixteen times. CORE-Bench shows a second,
complementary axis nobody else exploits: **three tasks from one capsule by information ablation alone**.

**Could scientific-chain grading complement final-deliverable grading?** This is where ForensicDS has something
APEX does not. APEX-Accounting's rubric rule is explicitly *"Outcome-based: Grading only the final answer, not
intermediate steps"*, and its judge sees the output **but not the trajectory**. Two benchmarks show what that
hides: **OpenRCA 2.0** grades a predicted causal propagation graph (Path Reachability / Node F1 / Edge F1) and
measures **AnySvc 76.0 % vs Path Reachability 61.5 % — a 14.5 pp gap of agents that named the right service
without being able to show why**, stating that *"outcome-only evaluation hides this failure mode"*. **BLADE**
grades the *decision set* with coverage over justifiable alternatives. Our phase-1 finding is the same shape
measured in a different domain: **6 of 13 failures reached the right decision with wrong quantities, and on G24 a
decision-only grader would have scored 3/3 instead of 0/3.** The safe implementation is OSWorld 2.0's: reward
**externalised intermediate artefacts** (files, logs, a written findings object the agent must produce), never
the chain-of-thought — OSWorld 2.0 found that state kept *"only in compressed reasoning or chain-of-thought
context"* is exactly where agents lose information, and that agents spend **under 7 %** of budget on
self-correction.

**Could our decision-only-grading discovery motivate richer evaluation?** That is the strongest single argument:
it is an empirical demonstration, on a professional task, that a benchmark grading only the deliverable would
have reported a solved task that was scientifically wrong in every trial.

**Could ForensicDS measure professional DS work current APEX domains do not cover?** APEX covers banking,
consulting, law, medicine, accounting and software engineering. **Quantitative/scientific analysis with a
decision attached is absent**, and APEX-Accounting's own failure analysis points at the gap: for top models
*"reasoning failures dominate: 79 % … 75 % … 59 % of annotated failures"*, with **"not a single annotated failure
involv[ing] tool use."* That is a measurement of the same thing this project is about, in a domain where the
scientific object is fixed by GAAP rather than something the analyst must derive.

**Three practices to borrow regardless.** (1) **Independent re-solve from the prompt and world files only** —
APEX-Accounting re-solved 20 tasks and found a **1.1 %** criterion defect rate; APEX-Agents re-solved 20 % and
**10 % needed fixes**. Our Oracle=1 proves *a* solution exists; it does not prove a competent human finds it from
the instruction, and this is the only instrument that separates "hard" from "under-specified". (2) **Report
pass^k.** (3) **Versioned maintenance with a published change log** — our `g36/v1_1/change_manifest.md` and
`development_defect_log.md` are already better artefacts than most published benchmarks produce; the governing
quote is APEX-Agents v1.1's *"Benchmarks are not static artifacts. They are software and we should maintain them
like software."*

## 34. UNCERTAINTIES

1. **Whether H1 survives at all**, and whether it survives on a second model family. External evidence cuts both
   ways: CLadder's ~50 % estimand derivation supports it; o1-mini's 87.9 F1 on ProcessBench and Gemini-2.5-Pro's
   I:C of 0.66 suggest the best models may be much less susceptible.
2. **Whether "discriminating test available and cheap" can be operationalised consistently by a second author**,
   or whether it is an author-dependent judgement. This is the single biggest threat to the slice's validity.
3. **Whether the decision-margin gate can be satisfied without making tasks feel knife-edge.** Phase 1 shows both
   failure modes: too wide and the decision survives everything; too narrow and the task grades precision.
4. **Whether three commitments per task is achievable without artificial stacking.** The first build will tell.
5. **Whether world-level artefacts help or leak.** A shared world could let an agent learn the world's habits in
   a way that substitutes for reasoning — and APEX's own within-world task independence is an inference from the
   Archipelago README, not a stated property.
6. **Whether a human expert baseline is affordable**, and without one, whether any claim about professional
   difficulty is defensible. TheAgentCompany and WorkArena++ both flag its absence as their main weakness.
7. **Whether grading a *diagnostic* (the A3 condition) can be done without prescribing a method**, and whether
   adding a findings artefact opens the hedging exploit faster than we can close it.
8. **How much of the phase-1 "no falsification" result is disposition and how much is the framing of our own
   instructions** (rival A8) — untested.
9. **Whether our resource limits bind.** Terminal-Bench 2.1 found 8 of 89 tasks had insufficient CPU/memory/time
   budgets, worth part of a 12-point capability understatement. We have never audited ours.
10. **Whether the L1/L2/L3 distinction survives contact with a real trajectory.** Phase 1's F11 label was added
    *after* the fact, and a pre-registered three-layer taxonomy may not partition cleanly.
11. **How much of any measured score is the scaffold.** Tool-interface choice alone moves TheAgentCompany by
    21.8–24.5 pp and scaffolds differ by ~44 pp on CORE-Bench; we have only ever run `gemini-cli`.

## 38. EXACT NEXT ACTION FOR CHATGPT

Everything needed to issue the implementation prompt for **task 1 = P22** without opening the repository.

**Context to state.** ForensicDS is a Harbor 0.21.0 benchmark of enterprise data-incident tasks, target model
`google/gemini-3-flash-preview`. Five tasks are frozen and shipped (Task02, G05, G10, G24, G34) with a further
two final tasks (G08, G41) and a development pool. Phase 1 measured 15 tasks / 45 valid trials and its audit
corrected the headline: the dominant failure is **commitment without a discriminating test** (F1/F4 with F7
secondary, plus the new **F11 coherence stopping**), not "right method, wrong object" (F9, 4/13 under a strict
bar). Across 45 trials there were **zero** placebo tests, pre-trend checks, negative controls, held-out
back-tests or known-answer simulations.

**The task to build.** *"Yield fell the week we recalibrated the CMM."* Discrete manufacturing, one plant, one
machined component.
- **Incident:** first-pass yield on a machined component drops **97.2 % → 92.8 % in week 19**; scrap is up
  **£180 k/month**; the supplier of the raw bar is being blamed.
- **Decision with a stated rule:** a supplier change worth **£2.4 m** and a possible line shutdown, governed by
  a threshold on the corrected nonconforming rate.
- **Hidden scientific issue:** the pass/fail outcome is produced by an **instrument whose bias changed** while
  the specification did not, so "yield" is not comparable across week 19.
- **Object to derive (stated nowhere in the workspace):** process capability and true nonconforming rate on a
  single measurement reference, with the measurement-bias component quantified and the material effect estimated
  after removing it.
- **The sophisticated wrong route the workspace must support:** a rigorous SPC and capability study — control
  charts by shift and material lot, Cp/Cpk before and after, and an ANOVA showing a significant lot effect —
  concluding the new heat lots are out of specification. It must **tie out**: charts in control apart from the
  shift, Cpk recomputes, lot means differ significantly.
- **Two discriminating routes, present but never named:** (1) **retained reference parts** measured on both
  machines read systematically ~**8 µm** larger, which alone moves the pass rate by most of the observed drop;
  (2) the **second, un-recalibrated CMM** shows no yield change on parts it measured over the same weeks.
- **Five evidence systems:** two CMMs' measurement logs · the calibration certificate (dated) · the retained
  reference-part archive · material certificates by heat · a functional test result set.
- **Graded quantities and tolerances:** measurement bias to **±1 µm**; corrected nonconforming rate to
  **±0.3 pp**; residual lot effect; the supplier decision. Accepted routes: reference-part bridge, second-machine
  comparison, or a regression bridge on the overlap — grade the bias and the corrected rate, never the method.
- **Three consequential commitments:** the measurement reference; variance allocation; the material effect after
  correction. The naive action (switch supplier) is **wrong**, so the decision cannot be accidentally right.
- **Ambiguity mitigations:** the drawing and the calibration procedure state which measurement is the
  specification reference; the certificate dates the change. The documents fix the question precisely and the
  answer not at all.
- **Cheap-solve status:** no single filter, join, formula or doc lookup reaches the answer — the certificate
  proves a recalibration happened, **not its magnitude or the corrected rate**. Residual risk: **low**.

**Non-negotiable build constraints to include in the prompt.**
1. **No target-model exposure during design or build.** No "quick test". A task shown to any target model is a
   discovery-set task for ever.
2. **Write the world specification and a trap register first** — every file, every task, every seeded
   contradiction enumerated before any file is authored.
3. **Record the expert solve path and the pre-registered analysis plan as dated documents before freeze.**
4. **The object must be stated nowhere.** An independent reviewer must search the workspace for any sentence
   stating it; if one exists, remove it or reject the task.
5. Harness shape: agent leaves a runnable command; the verifier re-runs it **unprivileged**
