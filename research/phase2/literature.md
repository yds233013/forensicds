# Phase 2 — scientific literature on falsification, self-correction and estimand reasoning

**Verdict on the project's working hypothesis.** "Models can produce convincing, internally coherent
reasoning without performing discriminating tests" is **well supported in a narrow, conditional form and
not as a blanket claim.** The defensible version: *absent an externally grounded signal, the mechanisms
that would discriminate good reasoning from bad — self-confidence, self-critique, step-level reward
models, self-consistency — are weakly to non-discriminative, and they degrade specifically on harder
problems, out of distribution, on self-generated content, and in domains without automatic verifiers.*
Nearly every method that works does so by importing information from **outside the chain being
evaluated**. That introspective-vs-externally-grounded distinction is the sharpest framing the evidence
carries.

Hygiene: items marked **[preprint]** are not peer-reviewed; several of the most on-point sources are
2025–26 preprints and are used to motivate the gap, not to establish a finding.

## The single strongest external support: estimand ≠ arithmetic

| source | finding | what it licenses |
|---|---|---|
| **CLadder** — Jin, Chen, Leeb, Gresele, Kamal, Lyu, Blin, Gonzalez Adauto, Kleiman-Weiner, Sachan, Schölkopf, **NeurIPS 2023**, arXiv:2312.04350 | step-wise F1: causal-graph extraction ≈**99 %**, arithmetic on the chosen estimand ≈**99 %**, **deriving the correct estimand ≈50 %**. Accuracy by rung: association 83.4 → intervention 67.5 → counterfactual 62.1 | the dissociation this project cares about, measured externally: computation is not the bottleneck, identification is |
| **QRData** — arXiv:2402.17644 | GPT-4 statistical 78.9 % vs **causal 46.8 %** (human graduate students 88.8/68.8). Error taxonomy over 50+ failures: knowledge recall 44 %, data analysis 34 %, equation formulation 16 %, **calculation 6 %** | failures are not arithmetic |
| backdoor-adjustment probe, arXiv:2410.11385 | **20–30 % for every model**, invariant to 0/1/2-shot, CoT and graph complexity, while factual inference on the same graphs reaches 83 % | prompt- and complexity-insensitivity is the signature of *not running the procedure* |
| **StatQA** — arXiv:2406.07815, **NeurIPS 2024 D&B** | "LLMs primarily make **applicability errors**, whereas humans mostly make statistical task confusion errors" | right method family, preconditions unchecked — the F1/F9 boundary in external data |
| **Corr2Cause** — Jin et al., **ICLR 2024** | finetuned models "fail in out-of-distribution settings generated by perturbing these queries": 94.74 F1 → 61.73 under paraphrase, → 67.87 under variable renaming | apparent competence can be shortcut learning |

## Self-correction without external feedback

- **Huang, Chen, Mishra, Zheng, Yu, Song, Zhou — "LLMs Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet", ICLR 2024,
  arXiv:2310.01798.** Intrinsic self-correction: GPT-4 GSM8K **95.5 → 91.5 → 89.0**; GPT-3.5 CommonSenseQA
  **75.8 → 38.1**. With **oracle correctness labels**: 75.9 → 84.3, 75.8 → 89.7, 49.0 → 59.0. At matched
  compute, plain self-consistency@9 (88.2) beats multi-agent debate (83.0).
- **Kamoi, Zhang, Zhang, Han, Zhang — TACL 2024, 12:1417.** "(1) no prior work demonstrates successful
  self-correction with feedback from prompted LLMs, except … tasks that are exceptionally suited …
  (2) self-correction works well in tasks that can use reliable external feedback, (3) large-scale
  fine-tuning enables self-correction." Documents oracle-label leakage in Reflexion and RCI, and weakened
  initial-generation prompts in Self-Refine.
- **Tyen, Mansoor, Cărbune, Chen, Mak — Findings of ACL 2024, arXiv:2311.08516.** Poor self-correction
  "stems from LLMs' **inability to find logical mistakes**, rather than their ability to correct a known
  mistake", and they struggle "even in highly objective, unambiguous cases"; given the error location,
  correction is robust. **A small out-of-domain classifier beats prompting a large model at detection.**
- **Olausson, Inala, Wang, Gao, Solar-Lezama — ICLR 2024:** self-repair gains are "modest, vary a lot …
  and are sometimes not present at all" once cost is counted; better *external* feedback dominates.
- **Stechly, Valmeekam, Kambhampati — arXiv:2402.08115 [preprint]:** "significant performance collapse
  with self-critique and significant performance gains with sound external verification."

## Does the model seek disconfirming evidence?

- **Jhaveri, GX-Chen, Sucholutsky, Choi — "Failing to Falsify", arXiv:2604.02485 [preprint].** Interactive
  Wason 2-4-6 plus a Blicket causal-discovery domain (192 episodes, 11 models). **Incompatible-to-Compatible
  test ratio 0.14–0.72 — every value below 1.0 means more confirming than disconfirming tests.** Lower
  confirmation bias correlates with higher discovery success; "think in opposites" / dual-goal prompts raise
  success **42 % → 56 %** and raise I:C in 18 of 22 model×condition cases. Toy domains; unreviewed.
- **Krishnamurthy, Harris, Foster, Zhang, Slivkins — NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2403.15371.** In-context bandit
  exploration: across 32 core prompt designs, **all but one** showed *suffix failure* (never choosing the
  best arm after some round) or *uniform-like failure*. The one success required **externally summarised**
  history.
- **Gandhi, Li, Goodyear, Bhatia, Li, Bhaskar, Zaman, Goodman — BoxingGym, arXiv:2501.01540.** Scores
  experiments by **expected information gain** against a known generative model; GPT-4o "struggle[s] with
  both experimental design and model discovery", and a novice agent given the scientist's verbal theory
  substantially underperforms the scientist — *the explanation does not carry the epistemic content the
  data did*.
- **Geng, Chen, Arumugam, Griffiths — arXiv:2505.17968.** "LLMs fail to extract information from
  observations, reaching a performance plateau that falls short of the ideal of Bayesian inference";
  prompting them to **intervene** helps, and the gain is partly attributable to *generating* the
  interventions. Failure modes named **overcomplication** and **overlooking**.
- **Laban, Hayashi, Zhou, Neville — arXiv:2505.06120.** **200,000+ simulated conversations**, six tasks:
  **39 % average drop** single-turn → multi-turn, decomposing into small aptitude loss and large
  unreliability gain. "Models make assumptions in early turns and prematurely attempt to generate final
  solutions, on which they overly rely"; "when LLMs take a wrong turn … they get lost and do not recover."
- **Riddell, Riddell, Sun, Antkiewicz, Czarnecki — FORGE 2026, arXiv:2601.22208.** The closest existing work
  to a professional setting (cloud root-cause analysis): **anchoring bias** ("fixates prematurely on one
  hypothesis and neglects exploration of alternatives") and **failure to update belief** are the strongest
  negative predictors of correctness (risk difference < −0.15, relative risk < 0.55). Caveat: small
  open-weight models only, LLM-as-judge annotation.

## Why the output is nonetheless convincing

- **Turpin, Michael, Perez, Bowman — NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2305.04388.** CoT explanations "can systematically
  misrepresent the true reason for a model's prediction"; accuracy drops up to **36 %** across 13 BBH tasks
  under biasing features the model never mentions.
- **Chen, Benton, Radhakrishnan, … Perez — arXiv:2505.05410 [preprint].** In synthetic RL environments with
  injected hacks, models reach **reward > 0.99** while verbalising the hack in **fewer than 2 %** of
  examples in 5 of 6 environments. Faithfulness is **44 % lower relatively on GPQA than MMLU** — it degrades
  where difficulty rises.
- **Si, Hashimoto, Yang — "The Ideation-Execution Gap", arXiv:2506.20803 [preprint].** 43 expert researchers
  each spent **100+ hours** executing a randomly assigned idea: LLM-generated ideas, rated **more novel and
  more exciting** than human ideas before execution, "decrease significantly more … on all evaluation
  metrics (novelty, excitement, effectiveness, and overall; p<0.05)" after it. Persuasiveness and empirical
  merit come apart, and only running the test reveals it.
- **Recchia et al. — arXiv:2507.19486 [preprint].** Human oversight study: "participants … become more
  confident in the system's answers after conducting online research, **even when those answers are
  incorrect**."
- **Farquhar, Kossen, Kuhn, Gal — Nature 630:625 (2024).** Semantic entropy detects confabulation at
  AUROC ≈0.790 — **and explicitly "does not help when LLM outputs are systematically bad"**. A confidently
  held, internally consistent, systematically wrong analysis has *low* semantic entropy and is invisible to
  consistency-based checking. This is the sharpest published statement that self-consistency cannot
  substitute for a discriminating test.
- **Xiong, Hu, Lu, Li, Fu, He, Hooi — ICLR 2024, arXiv:2306.13063.** Verbalised confidence discriminates a
  model's own correct from incorrect answers at **AUROC 0.522–0.605** — near chance — and all methods
  struggle on tasks requiring specialised knowledge.
- **Leng et al. — arXiv:2410.09724 [preprint].** Reward models "exhibit inherent biases towards
  high-confidence scores **regardless of the actual quality of responses**" — a training-time explanation
  for confident, untested output.

## Evidence that contradicts or complicates the hypothesis (must be reported)

1. **Process supervision works.** Lightman et al.: PRM **78.2** vs ORM 72.4 vs voting 69.6 at best-of-1860,
   with the gap **widening in N** and generalising out of distribution.
2. **Reasoning models are much better at error finding.** o1-mini reaches **87.9 avg F1** on ProcessBench
   against **56.5** for the best purpose-built process reward model.
3. **Training installs self-correction.** SCoRe (Kumar et al., arXiv:2409.12917): **+15.6 % MATH, +9.1 %
   HumanEval** from multi-turn RL on self-generated data — so "cannot self-correct" is a statement about
   prompted behaviour, not architecture.
4. **Models can beat humans at method selection.** StatQA: GPT-4o **64.83 %** vs stats-background humans
   **53.45 %**; a finetuned 8B model 77.13 %.
5. **The best models are much less biased.** Gemini-2.5-Pro: 73 % discovery success at I:C **0.66** in
   Jhaveri et al. Arcuschin et al.: in-the-wild post-hoc rationalisation **0.04 %** (Claude Sonnet 3.7 with
   thinking) and **0.37 %** (DeepSeek R1) versus up to 13 % for non-reasoning production models.
6. **Scale direction is unresolved.** Song et al.: the generation–verification gap **grows** with pretraining
   FLOPs (bigger models verify relatively better). Lanham et al.: faithfulness **falls** with scale. Both
   cannot be the whole story; name the tension rather than picking a side.
7. **Tools can make it worse.** NewtonBench's "tool paradox": a code interpreter can *hurt* capable models by
   triggering a premature shift from exploration to exploitation.
8. **Human baselines are not flattering.** DiscoveryWorld: 11 practising scientists reached ≈66 % task
   completion and ≈55 % explanatory knowledge. "Bad at falsification" and "worse than humans" are different
   claims.
9. **Faithfulness instruments are weak.** Gur-Arieh, Marasović, Geva, arXiv:2605.25052 [preprint]: most
   faithfulness metrics perform near chance; best 0.70 AUROC at CoT level, 0.59 at step level, and
   non-transferable. Treat published faithfulness effect sizes as provisional.

## The gap, stated precisely

> **No benchmark instruments, within a single agent trajectory and relative to the interpretation the agent
> currently holds, whether the next action was one whose outcome could have overturned that
> interpretation.**

The adjacent work covers three corners and nobody has put them together in a realistic setting:

| corner | who | limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **right construct** (confirming vs disconfirming test selection) | Jhaveri et al.'s I:C ratio | number triples and blickets — toy domains |
| **right formalism** (was this experiment capable of changing beliefs?) | BoxingGym's EIG, BED-LLM | measures uncertainty about the *true* model's parameters, not refutability of the *agent's own* belief |
| **right target** (justifiable alternative analyses, including controls) | BLADE's coverage@k | computed **across independent runs**, so it shows the output distribution is narrow, not that any single trajectory considered and ruled out an alternative; and BLADE states verbatim that it "does not evaluate an agent's ability to interpret the results" |

Additional unmeasured items relevant to this project: **target-leakage detection by LLM agents appears
genuinely unstudied** (the "LLM + leakage" literature is entirely benchmark contamination and multi-agent
privacy — two different senses of the word); **no benchmark for Simpson's paradox or collider/selection bias
at scale**; **abstention on non-identifiable questions is scored in essentially one place**; and the
"convincing" clause has been measured only on the human side (Recchia; Si et al.), never by taking an
agent's *data-analysis write-up* and measuring expert persuasion against methodological validity.

- "26.28 % of correct responses contain flawed reasoning";
- "25–28.5 % of audited code-RL tasks accept a wrong solution".
