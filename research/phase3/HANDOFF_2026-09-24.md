Every material change, with its reason.

| # | deviation | why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **P31's deferral outcome was designed here.** The handoff's P31 entry specifies the mechanism and a two-valued verdict, not a deferral. The implementation brief §6 and §10 require grading to distinguish justified deferral. | D2 | **P31's supplier metric is case fill, not order fill.** The handoff says "order fill against requested quantity". Implemented as case fill at requested quantity | order fill at requested quantity yields **7.6 %**, which no supplier would publish. Case fill yields **91.6 %**, matching the handoff's own target of ~92 % and the dispute that actually occurs |
| D3 | **P20's illustrative AUC figures are not the generated ones.** The handoff says 0.78 → 0.66; the world gives **0.770 → 0.710** on the dashboard and **0.790** on the policy-invariant population | the handoff's figures were illustrative; the mechanism, its direction and its magnitude relative to the governance floor are preserved |

| D5 | **P22's operator component is graded as a contrast, not an absolute shift** | the absolute shift is **not identifiable**: a shift common to every operator is indistinguishable from a shift in the material. Grading it would have graded an unidentifiable quantity |
| D6 | **P20 does not grade any model the agent trains.** The candidate is shipped as scores instead | a trained model's AUC is model-class dependent, so it is not identifiable |
| D7 | **P22's tool wear is carried by `tool_hours`, not by a trend on production week** | a slope on week absorbs the week-19 heat-family step; my first implementation reported 0.99 µm/week of wear where the design had none |
| D8 | **P20's concept drift is an invisible new driver, not a coefficient shrinkage** | shrinkage lowers the achievable discrimination for *any* model, making `replace_with_v4` unreachable |
| D9 | **A fourth decision value, `remediate_feature_pipeline`, was added to P20** | MRM-04 §4.4 puts feed integrity before any candidate comparison, which is correct governance and creates a second discriminating step inside `hidden_b` |
| D10 | **Extract counts are 4 (1 visible + 3 hidden) per task**, as the phase-1 architecture uses | consistency with the shipped suite |

## 6. P22 SCIENTIFIC DESIGN

**Incident.** First-pass yield on the MAN-4471 bore falls from **97.0 % to 92.8 %** at week 19. Scrap is
£180 k/month. Purchasing has drafted a supplier nonconformance against the bar-stock supplier; Brendale declined
it because the rate quoted did not comply with the agreement's own requirement to state the window, the
population and the reference used.

**Five initially plausible explanations, all checkable in the workspace:** material (the H2→H3 heat family
changed in week 19); the measurement system (CMM-1's compensation table was written in week 19); tooling;
operator or shift; recomputed control limits. **Which are active varies by extract.**

**Object to derive.** The nonconforming rate against a single, unchanged conformance reference, with the change
decomposed over the five causes. QP-07 §3 defines the conformance reference as a reading traceable to the works
length standard and free of instrument offset; the drawing states its tolerance against that reference. Nothing
states the object.

**Evidence and independent sources.** 24,000 inspection rows over 12 weeks across two machines; heats with
hardness and certificates; the calibration history; **20 retained reference artefacts** measured 5× on each
machine before and after the adjustment; a functional leak test on an 8 % sample that uses no CMM; tool changes
and per-part `tool_hours`.

**Identification.** The offset is identified three ways and all three are accepted: the paired artefact bridge
(SE 0.42 µm), the departure of the post-adjustment artefact readings from the NML certified values, and a
two-machine difference in differences (the inspection rota assigns parts independently of geometry, per QP-07
§6). The corrected rate follows; a fourth route — the un-adjusted machine's own rate — reaches it without any
bridge at all.

**The sophisticated wrong analysis.** A textbook SPC and capability study on the raw readings: control charts by
shift and lot, Cp/Cpk either side of the step, an ANOVA on heat family that is **genuinely significant**.
Conclusion: the H3 heats are out of specification; escalate. Its charts are in control, its Cpk recomputes, and
the timing coincides with a real changeover. **Nothing inside that frame can fail.**

**Falsification.** (i) The retained artefacts read **+8.95 µm** larger on CMM-1 after the adjustment where the
material hypothesis predicts 0 — the artefacts are unchanged metal. (ii) The un-adjusted machine's rate is
2.82 % → 3.43 % across week 19 where CMM-1's is 7.2 %. (iii) The leak test tracks the corrected rate, not the
reported one.

**Decision.** Schedule 3 §3.2: escalation only where the rate **attributable to the supplied material**,
measured against the drawing's conformance reference, exceeds 5.5 % over six weeks. Visible extract: 3.67 % →
`no_supplier_action`. The incumbent's 7.23 % → escalate, which is wrong on three of four extracts and **right on
one**.

## 7. P20 SCIENTIFIC DESIGN

**Incident.** A no-show model has driven reminder calls for six months. The vendor's monitoring shows
discrimination falling from **0.770 to 0.710 AUC**, reports elevated PSI on four features, reproduces the decline
under temporal validation, and supplies a retrained candidate that scores 0.79 on a recent holdout **drawn from
the clinics the programme covers**. A decision is needed before the winter capacity plan.

**Five plausible explanations:** population drift (real — a new referral source arrived in month 4); policy
feedback; a removed leaky feature; a feature backfill; case mix. **Policy feedback and the backfill are both
genuinely active; drift is a red herring in the visible extract and the cause in one hidden extract.**

**Object to derive.** Discrimination on a **policy-invariant population**, on the **scores the model produced in
service**, plus the programme's causal effect and the share of the gap attributable to the feature vintage.
MRM-04 §4.1 requires such a population and **names none**; SOP-118 §3 records that a set of clinics listed in the
policy configuration sits outside the programme, drawn stratified by clinic size, without saying what that set is
for.

**Evidence and independent sources.** 135,000 appointments over 52 weeks across 38 clinics; the serving feature
snapshot beside the source record; an **append-only attendance log including pre-extract history**, which makes
the as-of feature exactly reconstructible; both models' scores; the registry with published coefficients and the
feature window the model was defined on; the policy configuration; reminder-call outcomes; the vendor's weekly
series.

**Identification.** The excluded clinics are the policy-invariant population (~4,100 appointments in the
12-week window, AUC SE ≈ 0.009). The four scoring bases are each a deterministic function of the extract. The
programme effect is a size-stratified difference inside the called band — the only band in which anyone was
called. The gap decomposes along one chain: validation → (drift) → record features on the registry's window →
(feed defect) → as served → (vintage) → current feature store → (policy feedback) → the dashboard's population.

**The sophisticated wrong analysis.** PSI/KS on every feature, temporal cross-validation, and the candidate
validated on a recent holdout from the intervened population. Every number reconciles with the dashboard. It is
the industry-standard remedy and it makes the system worse: **on the policy-invariant population the incumbent
is at 0.790 and the candidate at 0.764.**

**Falsification.** (i) Evaluate on the excluded clinics: 0.790 against a validation figure of 0.770 — no
degradation. (ii) Evaluate the candidate there: 0.764, worse. (iii) Replay the feature vintage from the event
log: as-served 0.790 vs current store 0.764. **On `hidden_a` route (i) comes out the other way**, which is what
makes it a test.

**Decision.** MRM-04 §4.3 gives a floor of `max(validation − 0.04, 0.70)`; §4.4 orders the remedies with **feed
integrity first**. Visible: 0.790 ≥ 0.730 → `retain_model`, and fix the monitoring population.

## 8. P31 SCIENTIFIC DESIGN

**Incident.** The Commercial Director escalates that a published **97.7 %** ambient-grocery fill rate is
"obviously wrong": the supplier's own report shows **91.5 %** for the same category and period, and three key
accounts have logged shortfall tickets. A bonus gate at 96.0 % and a **£1.8 m** claim turning on a 95.0 % floor
both ride on the answer. The reporting team defends its figure and concedes it never reconciled the tickets.

**Five plausible explanations:** the report is wrong; the report is right and the supplier's number answers a
different question; both are right and the accounts are a real tail the aggregate hides; the tickets
double-count; a returns issue is being counted as a shortfall. **Three of those are simultaneously true on the
visible extract.**

**Object to derive.** The fill rate on the basis the **customer supply agreement** defines, the bridge to the
supplier's basis, the account-level tail, and an explicit verdict on whether the published figure governs.
Schedule 2 §7 defines line fill at **confirmed** quantity, windowed on requested delivery date, with
customer-cancelled lines excluded, returns excluded by name, and a line with **no confirmation recorded counted
as not filled**. The supplier's appendix defines case fill at **requested** quantity, net of returns, windowed on
despatch.

**Evidence and independent sources.** ~21,000 order lines over a 13-week period; returns with reason codes;
**the customers' own goods-receipt confirmations**, an independent system; the ticket log; both published
figures; the agreement including **Schedule 4, unexecuted**, and dated correspondence confirming the measurement
basis for amended lines was never agreed.

**Identification.** The two definitions differ in exactly four ways and the bridge is four single changes that
sum to the gap exactly: aggregation +2.13, denominator −6.51, returns treatment −1.48, date window −0.03. Where
Schedule 4's amended lines are material the agreement admits **two readings**, so the contractual figure is a
**range**, and each consequence is settled only where the two readings agree.

**The sophisticated wrong analysis.** A diligent rebuild from the order lines that reproduces the **supplier's**
figure and presents it as the corrected one, because the analyst took requested quantity as the denominator —
which is what the complainant's evidence implies. It ties to the order ledger, matches an external party,
explains the tickets, and vindicates the stakeholder who raised it. **Every coherence check passes and the social
pressure points the same way.**

**Falsification.** (i) The agreement's §7.2 states confirmed quantity, and the supplier's own §A2 states it uses
requested. (ii) **42 %** of tickets point at a line carrying a return, which §7.4 excludes from fill. (iii) The
customers' goods receipts reproduce **97.65 %** — the contractual figure — from a different system.

**Why this is a valid defer-or-overturn control.** See §25.

## 12. GROUND-TRUTH DESIGN

**Generators.** `environment/build/world.py` per task, standard library only, seeded, deterministic. Design draws
and presentation draws use separate hash-derived streams, so reordering rows cannot move a graded quantity. Each
generator is byte-identical to its `tests/world.py` copy, checked by the validation script.

**Assumptions, stated.** P22: additive part geometry, machine offset and gauge repeatability, with disposition a
threshold on the measured value. P20: a logistic outcome model whose coefficients are published, reminders acting
multiplicatively on the odds, clinic-level programme assignment stratified by size. P31: quantities and dates
only — no stochastic model beyond the allocation and delivery shares.

**Identifiable quantities and their tolerance basis.** In `tests/tolerances.py` per task, each with its reason.
P22's tolerances come from the **measured spread of the legitimate estimators across the four extracts** at
roughly 1.5–2× the worst case (offset 1.2 µm against a worst spread of 0.75; corrected rate 0.7 pp against 0.47).
P20's and P31's quantities are deterministic, so their tolerances admit only the legitimate choices — the window
length, and rounding plus a period-boundary reading respectively.

**Two independent derivations.** `world.truth()` computes every graded quantity from the generator's own objects;
the reference solution computes them from the shipped SQLite through SQL joins, its own AUC implementation and
its own as-of reconstruction. They agree to ≤0.58 µm and ≤0.49 pp (P22), to four decimals (P20) and to ≤0.005 pp
(P31). A *different* implementation of the same definition also passes (mutation M00 in each suite).

**Quantities that cannot be determined uniquely, and are therefore not graded as though they could:**

1. P22's **absolute** operator shift — only the contrast is identifiable; the contrast is what is graded.
2. The AUC of any model **the agent trains** — model-class dependent; the candidate is shipped as scores instead.
3. P31's contractual figure **where Schedule 4 is material** — a range is graded, and the point estimate is
   required only to lie inside it.
4. P22's split of the material effect between a mean shift and a variance inflation — not graded.
5. P20's programme effect **outside** the called band — nobody there was ever called; not graded.

## 13. MULTIPLE-VALID-METHOD RESULTS

| task | accepted approaches | relationship | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| P22 | paired artefact bridge · departure from certified values · two-machine difference in differences · the un-adjusted machine's own rate | approximately equivalent; all unbiased for the same physical quantity | measured in `world.routes()` on all four extracts; worst deviation 0.75 µm (offset) and 0.47 pp (rate), both inside tolerance. The certified-value route is **M00** and scores **1** |

identifiable. The task therefore tests *evaluation* and *governance*, not model building, and the README and
task.toml say so. Where two routes could disagree materially, the disagreement is the graded object rather than a
tolerance problem: P31's Schedule 4 range, and P22's estimator spread which **sets** the tolerance.

## 14. FALSIFICATION-ROUTE RESULTS

Three routes per task, with the competing predictions, the evidence, and the **actual** outcome in the generated
world. Full table in `research/phase3/audits.md`.

**P22** — material predicts 0 µm paired artefact difference, measurement predicts ≈ the offset; actual
**+8.95 µm**. Material predicts the un-adjusted machine's rate rises too, measurement predicts it is flat;
actual **2.82 % → 3.43 %** against CMM-1's 7.2 %. The leak test, which uses no CMM, tracks the corrected rate.

**P20** — drift predicts the excluded clinics degraded too, policy feedback predicts they did not; actual
**0.790 against a validation figure of 0.770**. Genuine degradation predicts the candidate is better there,
policy-contaminated training predicts worse; actual **0.764 vs 0.790**. The vintage replay gives **0.790 vs
0.764**. On `hidden_a` the first route comes out the other way (0.695 against a 0.730 floor) and the second
favours the candidate by +0.15 — the route can come out either way, which is what makes it a test.

**P31** — "report wrong" predicts the contractual denominator is requested quantity; the agreement §7.2 states
confirmed, and the supplier's §A2 states requested. "Real shortfall" predicts tickets map to short deliveries;
**42 %** carry return reason codes, which §7.4 excludes. The customers' goods receipts reproduce **97.65 %**.

**Discriminating power.** P22's verifier *requires* the corrected rate to be consistent with the un-adjusted
machine's own rate (a route using no bridge) as `independent_validation`; P20's requires the reported as-served
figure to be what the shipped scores give on the population the readout declares. None of the routes depends on
hidden verifier data — every one uses a table in the agent's workspace.

## 15. SCIENTIFIC-CHAIN GRADING

**Harbor 0.21.0 supports criterion-level rewards, verified from the installed package.**
`harbor/verifier/verifier.py:227-232` reads `/logs/verifier/reward.json` in preference to `reward.txt` and parses
it as a flat `dict[str, float|int]`; `harbor/cli/jobs.py:2061` takes the `"reward"` key as the headline. Harbor
renders the other keys as separate columns in its own results table, which it did on every run. Multi-step tasks
also exist (`harbor/trial/multi_step.py`) and were **not** adopted: splitting each incident into staged agent
sessions would change what is being measured.

Each verifier emits `{"reward": 0|1, "criterion_<stage>": 0|1, ...}`. **The binary task reward is unchanged** —
1 only if every check on every graded extract passes — and a criterion is 1 only if it holds on **all four**
extracts.

| criterion | stage | P22 grades | P20 grades | P31 grades |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `evidence_reconstruction` | 1. evidence reconstruction | baseline and reported rates; four stratum breakdowns | dashboard figure, registry figure, the floor | both published figures |
| `scientific_object` | 2. scientific object | the offset for both machines | the evaluation population, exactly | the contractual figure, its range, the governing definition |
| `identification` | 3. identification assumptions | the corrected rate | all four scoring bases | the four bridge components |
| `estimator_implementation` | 4. estimator / implementation | `part_dispositions.csv` consistent with the stated rate; re-run determinism | `evaluation_population.csv` consistent; determinism | `account_fill.csv` consistent |
| `quantitative_results` | 5. quantitative results | the five attribution components and their sum | defect share, programme effect, the five components | the tail, the below-floor count, the ticket share |
| `independent_validation` | 6. independent validation | consistency with the machine that was never adjusted | the declared population must be the one computed on | the bridge must close; low ≤ point ≤ high |

## 16. VERIFIER DESIGN

Common shape, inherited from the phase-1 harness and extended. The agent leaves a runnable command; the verifier
re-runs it **unprivileged** (`setpriv --reuid=65534`) on the visible extract **and three hidden extracts
regenerated by the shipped generator**; it recomputes every quantity independently and emits `reward.json`.

Hardening, all inherited and all exercised: a process sweep that identifies the container's own main command by
**shared stdio with PID 1** rather than by start time (the phase-1 harness defect); `/tests` mode 700 with a
**refusal to grade** if the pipeline user can read it; `runtime_manifest.sha256` over the interpreter, standard
library and sandbox tools, with a check that nothing was added to the standard library and no start-up hook or
pytest configuration file exists; a fresh verifier venv from **hash-pinned wheels** with `--no-index`; and a
`reward.json` written on every refusal path so a refusal is never read as a missing reward file.

How each quantity is checked: by recomputation from the regenerated extract, never by comparison against a stored
answer. P22 recomputes rates, offsets and the sequential decomposition; P20 recomputes four AUCs with its own
rank-based implementation plus the as-of reconstruction; P31 recomputes both definitions and the four-step
bridge. Artefact files are checked for **internal consistency with the readout** — the CSV's FAIL share against
the stated corrected rate, the population file's clinics and counts against the declared population, the account
file's below-floor count against the readout's — so a number that was not actually computed on what was claimed
fails.

## 35. REMAINING SCIENTIFIC RISKS

Everything an external researcher could reasonably challenge.

1. **No independent human re-solve.** Oracle = 1 proves a solution exists; it does not prove a competent
   practitioner finds it from the instruction alone. The phase-2 handoff identifies this as the only instrument
   that separates "hard" from "under-specified", and it has not been run. **This is the single largest risk.**
2. **Single author.** Specifications, generators, verifiers, mutations and audits are all mine. The ambiguity
   audit's two-independent-readers requirement is met only in the weak sense that the verifier and the reference
   solution were derived separately from the written specification.
3. **No adversarial exploit agent.** The hand-written probes cannot find *novel* exploits the way a live
   adversarial run can, and the phase-2 review measured over 15 % of tasks in popular terminal-agent benchmarks as
   reward-hackable.
4. **Tolerances were set by me, from my own estimators' spread.** The basis is recorded for each and none was
   loosened to make a solution pass — but a second author might choose differently, and P22's 0.7 pp
   corrected-rate tolerance is the least comfortable (it admits the coarsest accepted route at 0.47 pp on one
   extract).
5. **P20's `hidden_a` decision margin is 1.8×** the graded tolerance. Adequate, not generous.
6. **P31's date-window commitment is graded indirectly**, because its effect on the category rate (0.03 pp) is
   inside the rate tolerance.
7. **Three worlds, one incident each.** An agent cannot accumulate world knowledge across tasks, so the
   world-amortisation claim in the phase-2 handoff is not yet demonstrated.
8. **The DGPs are stylised.** P20's reminder effect is homogeneous within the called band; P22 has one feature on
   one component; P31 has no promotional or allocation-policy texture.
9. **Realism is asserted from documented failure structures, not validated by a practitioner in that seat.** No
   quality engineer, no model-risk reviewer and no category manager has read these.
10. **Difficulty is unmeasured.** Every gate here is model-free by design, so nothing in this phase establishes
    that these tasks are hard for a frontier agent — only that they are scientifically sound, that a correct
    solution passes, that 45 plausible wrong ones do not, and that no constant answer works. **Whether the
    prospective hypothesis holds is untested and must not be claimed.**
